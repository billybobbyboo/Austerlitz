## Claims

### 1. Line infantry (fusiliers, grenadiers, voltigeurs)

- **id**: `fr.line.headgear.decree1806`
- **claim**: Bardin states that the decree of 25 February 1806 gave the shako to all the infantry of the army except the Guard; before it, the line infantry's headgear was the hat (chapeau), whose suppression for the line was "signalled" by the shako issued to the grenadiers réunis at Arras in 1804.
- **value (source's words)**: "chapeau des troupes de ligne"; "Le décret de 1806 (25 février) donnait le Schako à toute l'infanterie de l'armée, la garde exceptée"
- **source**: bardin15_1850, article "SCHAKO D'INFANTERIE", p. 4789, right column (archive.org leaf n314), https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_wYFsiLtmMuEC/page/n314
- **quote**: "En 1804, le corps des grenadiers réunis à Arras recevait le Schako. Ce fut le signal de l'abolition de la chevelure poudrée et de la suppression du chapeau des troupes de ligne; [...] — Le décret de 1806 (25 février) donnait le Schako à toute l'infanterie de l'armée, la garde exceptée, [...]. Ce Schako n'était plus à flamme, mais simple et à visière." (transcribed from the page image; the djvu OCR reads "DECHET DF 1800 (25 févriee)" [sic])
  Translation: "In 1804 the corps of grenadiers réunis at Arras received the shako. This was the signal for the abolition of powdered hair and the suppression of the hat of the line troops; [...] The decree of 1806 (25 February) gave the shako to all the infantry of the army, the Guard excepted [...]. This shako no longer had a flamme, but was plain and with a peak."
- **grade**: B. Specialist work (Bardin, a general of the period, d. 1840; posthumous edition) describing the legislation; the decree is quoted in Berriat (`fr.line.headgear.decree1806.text`).
- **label**: fact (for what Bardin states); the conclusion that line fusiliers still wore the hat on 2 December 1805 is **inference** from the decree's date (a February 1806 decree "giving" the shako implies it was not yet general regulation), not a statement of what every battalion wore on the day.
- **notes**: Bardin does not say how quickly the 1806 decree was carried out, nor whether some line regiments had shakos earlier on their own initiative. The 1804 Arras date bears on Oudinot's grenadiers (see section 4).

- **id**: `fr.line.headgear.hat`
- **claim**: Bardin states that the line infantry ("infanterie de bataille") gave up the hat for the shako between 1804 and 1806, the light infantry having adopted the shako earlier; and that the shako replaced the hat in the line infantry "since 1806", while the ministry's tariffs of year XI (1802-03) still listed the hat.
- **value (source's words)**: "Chapeau"; "schako"
- **source**: bardin4_1849, article "CHAPEAU", p. 1159 left column (leaf n206), https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_xJVcg1iO_pQC/page/n206 ; article "CHAPEAU DE TROUPE", p. 1161 (leaf n208), https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_xJVcg1iO_pQC/page/n208
- **quote**: (p. 1159, checked against page image) "De 1804 à 1806, le Chapeau est abandonné par notre infanterie de bataille pour faire place au schako adopté plus anciennement déjà par notre infanterie légère." / (p. 1161, OCR) "Les tarifs ministériels de l'an onze font encore mention du Cha- peau. — Depuis la fin du dernier siècle, les scbaros [sic] ont remplacé les Chapeaux dans I'infantrrif. légère [sic]; et depuis 1806 ils les ont remplacés dans I'inpanterie de bataille dr ugne [sic]"
  Translation: "From 1804 to 1806 the hat was given up by our line infantry to make way for the shako, adopted earlier by our light infantry." / "The ministerial tariffs of year XI still mention the hat. Since the end of the last century shakos have replaced hats in the light infantry, and since 1806 in the line infantry."
- **grade**: B. Specialist work; describes a transition spread over 1804-1806 without saying which line regiments had changed by December 1805.
- **label**: fact (per Bardin); for 2 December 1805 the line fusiliers' headgear is **uncertain between hat and shako for individual regiments**, with the hat the regulation headgear until the decree of 25 February 1806 (inference from `fr.line.headgear.decree1806`).
- **notes**: Bardin's "De 1804 à 1806" leaves room for some line units in shakos before the decree (the grenadiers réunis in 1804 per p. 4789). No regiment-by-regiment evidence was read. The project's current generic shako on every French figure is therefore not supported for line fusiliers as the default.

- **value (source's words)**: "l'une de ses grandes cornes en avant"

- **id**: `fr.line.headgear.decree1806.text`
- **claim**: The imperial decree of 25 February 1806, printed in a collection of texts in force, provides that the shako shall be the headgear of the line infantry "from the renewal of 1807"; Berriat's note extends the provision to all arms except cuirassiers, carabiniers and dragoons.
- **value (source's words)**: "le schakos sera la coiffure de l'infanterie de ligne"
- **source**: berriat3_1812, no. 467, p. 227 (archive.org leaf n241), https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_Ic0GiuKgGOYC/page/n241
- **quote**: (from page image) "N.° 467. Décret impérial, portant qu'à dater du renouvellement de 1807 le schakos, sera la coiffure de l'infanterie de ligne. Du 25 février 1806. A dater du renouvellement de 1807 le schakos sera la coiffure de l'infanterie de ligne (3)." Note (3): "Ces dispositions sont applicables à toutes les armes à l'exception, des cuirassiers, des carabiniers et des dragons."
  Translation: "Imperial decree providing that from the renewal of 1807 the shako shall be the headgear of the line infantry. Of 25 February 1806. From the renewal of 1807 the shako shall be the headgear of the line infantry. Note: these provisions apply to all arms except the cuirassiers, carabiniers and dragoons."
- **grade**: A for the decree's text and date (primary text, reprinted verbatim in an official-style collection). For 2 December 1805 the claim "the shako was not yet the line infantry's regulation headgear" is **derived** from the decree (a 1806 act making it the headgear only from 1807), grade B.
- **label**: fact (decree); derived (state in December 1805)
- **notes**: The decree confirms the date reported by Bardin (`fr.line.headgear.decree1806`). It says nothing about line regiments that adopted shakos before 1806 on their own account; Bardin's "De 1804 à 1806" leaves that open. Corroboration: the regulation of 10 February 1806 in the same volume (no. 466, p. 227 top) still lists the replacement items of the men as "l'habit, de la veste et du chapeau" (coat, waistcoat and hat), from page image.

- **id**: `fr.line.headgear.reglt1806.items`
- **claim**: The regulation of 10 February 1806 on the masse générale names the hat ("chapeau"), with coat and waistcoat, among the clothing items whose replacement it regulates for the men of all arms' corps of new formation.
- **value (source's words)**: "le remplacement de l'habit, de la veste et du chapeau"
- **source**: berriat3_1812, no. 466, p. 227 (leaf n241), https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_Ic0GiuKgGOYC/page/n241
- **quote**: (from page image) "Mais les hommes qui auront reçu l'habillement neuf la première année, ne recevront le remplacement de l'habit, de la veste et du chapeau, qu'après deux années révolues."
  Translation: "But the men who receive new clothing in the first year shall receive the replacement of coat, waistcoat and hat only after two full years."
- **grade**: A as a text dated February 1806, ten weeks after the battle (for the regulation's item list); its application to the line infantry on 2 December 1805 is inference.
- **label**: fact (text); inference (that the hat was the standard headgear item of the infantry administration in winter 1805-06)
- **notes**: The regulation concerns administration, not dress; it does not distinguish arms.

- **id**: `fr.line.gaiters.rule`
- **claim**: The police regulation of 24 June 1792 (service intérieur), reprinted in 1813 as in force, requires infantry NCOs and soldiers on duty to be always in black gaiters and, on the march, in grey linen gaiters; the soldiers wear at all times the coat, waistcoat and breeches of their regiment, and a black stock (white only on days of grand parade). Bardin's note adds that the linen gaiters were blackened while the infantry was dressed in white, and worn in their natural colour only since the adoption of the blue coat.
- **value (source's words)**: "toujours en guêtres noires : dans les marches, ils porteront des guêtres de toile grise"; "le col noir"; "l'habit, la veste et la culotte (pantalon) uniformes affectés à leur régiment"
- **source**: bardin_memorial1813, no. 106 ("Règlement de police du 24 juin 1792"), alinéas 182-190, p. 157 and note (4) (archive.org leaf n194, checked against page image), https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_BmAOAAAAQAAJ/page/n194
- **quote**: (from page image) "11. Col. Les sous-officiers et soldats porteront habituellement le col noir [...]. 12. Habillement. Les sous-officiers et soldats porteront en tout temps l'habit, la veste et la culotte (pantalon) uniformes affectés à leur régiment. [...] 14. Tenue de service. Les sous-officiers et soldats de service seront toujours en guêtres noires : dans les marches, ils porteront des guêtres de toile grise (4)." Note (4): "Aussi long-temps que toute l'infanterie a été vêtue en blanc, les guêtres de toile ont été noircies et cirées; on ne les a porté de couleur naturelle que depuis la prise de l'habit bleu."

- **id**: `fr.line.coat.colour`
- **claim**: Bardin's Dictionnaire states that the blue coat was adopted for the infantry in February 1793, at the formation of the demi-brigades (white coats long remaining in use for lack of money), and that it was only in 1806 that Napoleon decided to return the infantry to the white coat (dispositions of 25 April and 24 July 1806; a third of the regiments received a year's replacements in white cloth; suppressed 26 June 1807).
- **value (source's words)**: "l'habit bleu est adopté dans l'infanterie"; "habit de drap bleu national, qui fut maintenu jusqu'en 1806"; "rendre à l'infanterie l'habit blanc"
- **source**: bardin17_1851, article "UNIFORME", pp. 5160-5161 (leaves n47-n48; p. 5161 checked against page image), https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_iGVBRA6sQTwC/page/n48
- **quote**: (p. 5160, OCR) "Au mois de février 1793 [OCR 'l7Uô'], épo- que de ta formation des demi-brigades . I'hamt bleu [sic] est adopté dans l'infatHerie [sic]; mais le mauvais état <ies [sic] finances laisse voir encore lnngtem|is [sic] des habits blancs dans rarnièr [sic]." / (p. 5161, from page image) "Tous devaient porter l'habit de drap bleu national, qui fut maintenu jusqu'en 1806. A cette époque, Napoléon, séduit par des projets d'économie, [...] eut l'idée de rendre à l'infanterie l'habit blanc: un tiers des régiments de l'armée reçut les remplacements d'une année en drap de cette couleur. Tel fut l'objet des dispositions du 25 avril et du 24 juillet. [...] mais l'habit blanc fut supprimé le 26 juin 1807."
  Translation: "In February 1793, when the demi-brigades were formed, the blue coat was adopted in the infantry; but the poor state of finances left white coats in the army for a long time." / "All were to wear the coat of national blue cloth, which was kept until 1806. At that time Napoleon, attracted by plans for economy, [...] had the idea of returning the infantry to the white coat: a third of the regiments received one year's replacements in cloth of that colour. Such was the purpose of the dispositions of 25 April and 24 July. [...] but the white coat was suppressed on 26 June 1807."
- **grade**: B. Specialist work; the 1806 white-coat dispositions are dated after Austerlitz, so the line infantry coat in December 1805 was blue by Bardin's chronology (derived).
- **label**: fact (per source); **derived**: line infantry coats were national blue on 2 December 1805.
- **notes**: "Tous" in the p. 5161 sentence refers grammatically to the officers of the year-XII regulation; the infantry's blue before 1806 follows from "rendre à l'infanterie l'habit blanc" in 1806 and the 1793 adoption. No facing colours (collar, lapels, cuffs, turnbacks) for the 1805 line infantry were found stated in Bardin (see Not found). The same article (p. 5161) says part of the light infantry took "de son propre mouvement [...] l'habit court et le pantalon" (of its own accord, the short coat and trousers) around year VI, later legalised.

### 12. Greatcoats (capote) and cloaks (manteau)

- **id**: `fr.greatcoat.infantry.status`
- **claim**: Bardin states that greatcoats were first issued in 1792 (Army of Belgium); that French greatcoats were at first "of all colours", issued to certain troops as a gratuity and a means of campaigning; that many corps still had none in year XII (1803-04), when the ministry's instructions called it the "capote de campagne" and ordered the greatcoats of corps not on campaign to be put into store.
- **value (source's words)**: "capote de campagne"; "de toutes couleurs"

- **quote**: (checked against page image) "Les Capotes françaises ont d'abord été de toutes couleurs; elles étaient délivrées à certaines troupes comme gratification et comme un moyen de faire campagne; mais il y avait beaucoup de corps qui n'en avaient pas; il en était encore ainsi en l'an douze; aussi les instructions ministérielles l'appellent-elles capote de campagne, et veulent-elles qu'on emmagasine celles des corps qui ne font pas ou qui ne font plus campagne."
  Translation: "French greatcoats were at first of all colours; they were issued to certain troops as a gratuity and as a means of campaigning; but many corps had none; it was still so in year XII; hence the ministerial instructions call it the campaign greatcoat and require that the greatcoats of corps not, or no longer, on campaign be put in store."

- **notes**: Bardin also says (same page) that some corps wore the greatcoat rolled "en sautoir" (bandolier fashion, right to left), and that a later mode carried it on the knapsack; neither is dated.
