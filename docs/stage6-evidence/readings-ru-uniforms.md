## Claims
### A. Army infantry (grenadier and musketeer regiments): coat, legwear, greatcoat

Locator convention: `viskovatov10_1900` page as printed / archive.org leaf `n<k>` (the page image at
`https://archive.org/download/istoricheskoeopisanieodezhdyivooruzhenij12/page/n<k>.jpg`). Quotes are transcribed from the page
image in the printed (pre-1918) orthography; the archive.org OCR of the same pages has errors, so a check against the OCR will
differ letter by letter. Viskovatov's dates are Old Style (12 days behind the Gregorian calendar in 1805: 2 December 1805 NS =
20 November 1805 OS; derived).

- **id**: ru.inf.coat
- **claim**: By the table of 30 April 1802 the grenadier private's coat (mundir) was double-breasted, dark-green cloth, with a standing collar of a colour set per Inspection, cuffs of the collar's colour with dark-green cuff flaps, red kersey lining, brass buttons and two shoulder straps of a colour set per regiment within the Inspection.
- **value (source's words)**: "мундиръ ... двубортный, изъ темнозеленаго сукна"; collar "въ каждой Инспекціи особаго цвѣта"; "погонами, въ каждомъ полку Инспекціи особаго цвѣта"
- **source**: viskovatov10_1900, p. 196 (leaf n199); plate 1274
- **quote**: "Мундиръ полагался двубортный, изъ темнозеленаго сукна, съ стоячимъ воротникомъ, въ каждой Инспекціи особаго цвѣта; съ обшлагами по цвѣту воротника; съ темнозелеными клапанами, на обшлагахъ; съ красною, каразейною подкладкою, съ мѣдными пуговицами и съ двумя погонами, въ каждомъ полку Инспекціи особаго цвѣта." (The coat was to be double-breasted, of dark-green cloth, with a standing collar of a particular colour in each Inspection; with cuffs of the collar's colour; with dark-green flaps on the cuffs; with red kersey lining, with brass [copper-alloy] buttons and with two shoulder straps of a particular colour in each regiment of the Inspection.)
- **grade**: B (a specialist work, 1851, describing the 1802 table from the regulations, PSZ cited in its notes 665-669, p. 293; Viskovatov records no change to the coat's colour or cut for these regiments between 1802 and 1 November 1807 except the Dniester Inspection's of 15 November 1804, see ru.inf.facings; that absence is the only evidence it was still in force, so not A)
- **label**: fact
- **notes**: the same pages extend the table to musketeer regiments (ru.mus.base). Dark green is the regulation colour; no shade value is given.

- **id**: ru.inf.facings
- **claim**: In 1802-1807 the facing system was: collar and cuffs (and the grenadier cap's crown) in the Inspection's colour; shoulder straps (and the grenadier cap's back band) in a colour per regiment within the Inspection; the 15 November 1804 order changed the Dniester Inspection's grenadier and musketeer regiments to dark-green collars and cuff flaps piped red, with red cuffs.
- **value (source's words)**: Inspection colours read in the list of 6 June 1802 include "красные" (St Petersburg Inspection grenadiers), "бирюзовые" (Livonia), "свѣтлозеленые" (Lithuania), "розовые" (Ukraine), "лиловые" (Dniester), "синіе" (Caucasus), "бѣлые" (Smolensk), "оранжевые" (Moscow); for musketeers also "желтые" (Finland), "сѣрые" (Siberia), "верблюжіе" and others
- **source**: viskovatov10_1900, pp. 204-205 (leaves n207-n208), grenadier list; pp. 230-241 (leaves n233-n244), musketeer list; p. 207 (leaf n210) and p. 242 (leaf n245) for 15 November 1804
- **quote**: (grenadiers, p. 205) "Смоленской Инспекціи въ Фанагорійскомъ Гренадерскомъ полку: Воротникъ и обшлага бѣлые; погоны красные (Рис. № 1287); верхушки гренадерскихъ и фузелерныхъ шапокъ бѣлыя, а околышки красные" [page image checked] (Smolensk Inspection, Phanagoria Grenadier Regiment: collar and cuffs white; shoulder straps red; crowns of the grenadier and fusilier caps white, and the bands red). (Musketeers, p. 230) "Той же Инспекціи въ Рязанскомъ полку: Воротникъ, обшлага и погоны желтые; верхушки гренадерскихъ шапокъ и околыши желтые" [page image checked, leaf n233] (Same [Finland] Inspection, in the Ryazan regiment: collar, cuffs and shoulder straps yellow; crowns and bands of the grenadier caps yellow.)
- **grade**: B (specialist description of the order of 6 June 1802 and the table of 30 April 1802; whether changed for a given regiment before late 1805 must be checked regiment by regiment in Viskovatov's later entries, e.g. 3 August 1804, p. 242)
- **label**: fact (for the system as Viskovatov describes it); see Disagreements for Gingerich's reported objection (not read)
- **notes**: the project's regiments' individual colours are not needed by the brief; only the assignment rule is recorded here. The 15 Nov 1804 entry (p. 207 and p. 242) applies only to the Dniester Inspection (Kherson and Siberia Grenadiers; Nizhegorod, Vladimir, Yaroslavl, Aleksopol, Kozlov, New Ingermanland, Crimea Musketeers).

- **id**: ru.inf.legwear
- **claim**: The 1802 table gave the private white woollen-cloth trousers (pantalony), of Flemish linen in summer, ending 5 vershok above the heel, worn with greased (смазные) round-toed boots reaching 3 vershok above the trousers' lower edge (i.e. short boots under long trousers buttoned to them).
- **value (source's words)**: "Панталоны, изъ бѣлаго сукна, а лѣтомъ изъ фламскаго полотна"; "Сапоги, смазные, круглоносые"
- **source**: viskovatov10_1900, p. 197 (leaf n200)
- **quote**: "Панталоны, изъ бѣлаго сукна, а лѣтомъ изъ фламскаго полотна,— въ длину были, отъ верхняго края портупеи, въ 1 арш. 5½ вершковъ, т. е. на 5 вершковъ не доходили до каблука" (Trousers, of white cloth, and in summer of Flemish linen, were in length, from the upper edge of the sword belt, 1 arshin 5½ vershok, i.e. they stopped 5 vershok short of the heel)
- **grade**: B (as ru.inf.coat; the next change Viskovatov records is 23 December 1807, winter trousers with leather trim, p. 210, i.e. after the battle)
- **label**: fact
- **notes**: 1 vershok = 4.445 cm (derived; standard Russian measure), so 5 vershok ≈ 22 cm. The same page (image checked): "Сапоги, смазные, круглоносые,—вышиною, отъ каблука вверхъ, въ 8 вершковъ, т. е. тремя вершками выше нижняго края панталонъ" (boots, greased, round-toed, 8 vershok high from the heel, i.e. three vershok above the lower edge of the trousers). Whether white trousers were worn on campaign in November 1805 is not stated here (see ru.officers.march for officers' grey overalls).

- **id**: ru.inf.greatcoat
- **claim**: The 1802 greatcoat (shinel) was of undyed cloth, dark or light grey but uniform within a regiment, with collar and shoulder straps in the coat's colours and cut, round grey cuffs, seven flat brass buttons, cut to go over the coat and the sheepskin warm coat.
- **value (source's words)**: "Шинель,—изъ некрашенаго сукна, темно или свѣтлосѣраго, только бы во всемъ полку одинаковаго, цвѣта"
- **source**: viskovatov10_1900, p. 198 (leaf n201); plate 1276
- **quote**: "Шинель,—изъ некрашенаго сукна, темно или свѣтлосѣраго, только бы во всемъ полку одинаковаго, цвѣта,—съ воротникомъ и погонами по цвѣту и покрою мундирныхъ, и съ сѣрыми, круглыми обшлагами,—строилась такъ, что могла быть надѣваема не только на мундиръ, но еще и на фуфайку или полушубокъ." (Greatcoat, of undyed cloth, dark or light grey, provided only that it was of one colour throughout the regiment, with collar and shoulder straps in the colour and cut of the coat's, and with grey round cuffs, was made so that it could be worn not only over the coat but also over the warm coat or half-length sheepskin.)
- **grade**: B (regulation of 1802 via a specialist work; the sheepskin "фуфайки" were abolished only on 1 October 1806, p. 208, so the 1802 issue was still current in 1805: inference from the chronology)
- **label**: fact
- **notes**: whether greatcoats were worn in the battle is the eyewitness agent's question; Viskovatov's text read here says nothing of 1805 wear.

### B. Army infantry headgear (the key question)

- **id**: ru.gren.cap1802
- **claim**: The 1802 grenadier cap of the grenadier battalions was of almost the same form and size as under Paul I: a brass front plate stamped almost over its whole height with the double-headed eagle bearing St George, three brass grenades at back and sides, the crown in the collar's colour and the back band in the shoulder straps' colour, black edging, white tape trim on the crown, tassels by a list.
- **value (source's words)**: "Гренадерская шапка ... съ бляхою изъ мѣдной латуни, напереди; съ тремя мѣдными же гренадами"
- **source**: viskovatov10_1900, pp. 197-198 (leaves n200-n201); plate 1275 (cap of the Pavlov Grenadiers, captioned 1802-1813 in conrad_v10b's list)
- **quote**: "Гренадерская шапка,—почти той же формы и величины, какъ и при Императорѣ Павлѣ І-мъ, именно: съ бляхою изъ мѣдной латуни, напереди; съ тремя мѣдными же гренадами, назади и по бокамъ,—имѣла на первой, т. е. на бляхѣ, почти во всю ея величину, вычеканенное изображеніе двуглаваго, Россійскаго Орла, съ Св. Георгіемъ на груди; верхушку—по цвѣту воротника и обшлаговъ, а задникъ или околышъ—по цвѣту погоновъ." [page images checked, leaves n200-n201] (The grenadier cap, of almost the same form and size as under Emperor Paul I, namely with a plate of brass in front, with three grenades also of brass at the back and sides, had on the first, i.e. the plate, almost over its whole size, a stamped image of the double-headed Russian eagle with St George on its breast; the crown in the colour of the collar and cuffs, and the back or band in the colour of the shoulder straps.)
- **grade**: B for 1802-1804 (regulation via specialist work). For 2 December 1805: C, because Viskovatov records its replacement ordered on 13 February 1805 (ru.gren.shako1805); whether the replacement had reached the regiments is not shown (see ru.inf.headgear.issue).
- **label**: fact (for the 1802 regulation); disputed (for wear in late 1805)

- **id**: ru.mus.hat1802

- **value (source's words)**: "треугольныя шляпы" (the source's own term: triangular hats, not "двууголка")
- **source**: viskovatov10_1900, pp. 229-230 (leaves n232-n233); plate 1344 (Velikie Luki Musketeer, "1802 and 1803")
- **quote**: "...распространялись, въ равной силѣ, и на полки Мушкетерскіе, съ тою только разницею, что въ сихъ послѣднихъ всѣ нижніе строевые чины Мушкетерскихъ баталіоновъ и полковые барабанщики, вмѣсто шапокъ, носили треугольныя шляпы. Сіи послѣднія были совершенно подобны тѣмъ шляпамъ, какія, въ то же время, были даны нестроевымъ Гренадерскихъ полковъ, но только съ прибавленіемъ кокарды, двухъ кисточекъ по угламъ и одной кисточки надъ кокардою (Рис. № 1344)." (...applied with equal force to the musketeer regiments, with this difference only, that in the latter all the combatant lower ranks of the musketeer battalions and the regimental drummers wore triangular hats instead of caps. - **label**: fact (for 1802-1803)

- **id**: ru.mus.cap1803
- **claim**: On 19 August 1803 all musketeer lower ranks who had been given hats received instead caps (шапки) of the same pattern as the grenadier non-combatants' of that date: a round cap 4½ vershok tall, of black cloth, with ear and cheek flaps for frost, a black lacquered leather peak, a black leather chin strap, a black cockade edged orange with a brass button, two woollen tassels above it in the colours of 6 June 1802, stiffened with upright straw and quilted, the lower edge bound in black leather; NCOs with gold lace round the top.
- **value (source's words)**: "даны шапки, одного образца съ полученными, въ это время, нестроевыми Гренадерскихъ полковъ"; the pattern: "круглыя шапки, въ 4½ вершка вышиною; изъ чернаго сукна"
- **source**: viskovatov10_1900, p. 242 (leaf n245) for the musketeers; p. 206 (leaf n209) for the pattern; plates 1293-1294 and 1356 ("Musketeer NCO 1803-1805": a tall cylindrical black cap with a peak, cockade, tassels and lace at the top, viewed on leaf n364)
- **quote**: (p. 242) "19 Августа 1803 — всѣмъ нижнимъ чинамъ Мушкетерскихъ полковъ, коимъ полагались шляпы, даны шапки, одного образца съ полученными, въ это время, нестроевыми Гренадерскихъ полковъ (Рис. № 1356) (756)." (p. 206) "19 Августа 1803—нижнимъ нестроевымъ чинамъ Гренадерскихъ полковъ, вмѣсто треугольныхъ шляпъ, даны круглыя шапки, въ 4½ вершка вышиною; изъ чернаго сукна, съ такими же двумя лопастями, пришитыми изъ-внутри и служившими для закрытія въ морозы ушей и щекъ; съ лакированнымъ козырькомъ, изъ черной кожи" (19 August 1803: the non-combatant lower ranks of grenadier regiments were given, instead of triangular hats, round caps 4½ vershok tall, of black cloth, with two flaps sewn inside serving to cover the ears and cheeks in frost; with a lacquered peak of black leather)
