## Claims

### Weather on the day (as it bears on dress)

- **id**: `weather.night1to2.cold-fog`
- **claim**: An aide-de-camp at imperial headquarters records that on the night of 1-2 December there was no snow, it was very cold, and a thick fog darkened the night.
- **value (source's words)**: "point de neige", "très froid", "épais brouillard"
- **source**: marbot1891, t. I, p. 258 (leaf 282), https://archive.org/details/mmoiresdugnralba01marb/page/258/mode/1up
- **quote**: "Heureusement, il n'y avait point de neige, et quoiqu'il fit très froid, je me couchai sur la terre et m'endormis profondément [...] Il n'y avait point de lune, et l'obscurité de la nuit était augmentée par un épais brouillard qui rendait la marche fort difficile." (OCR; "fit" for "fît" as printed in OCR) / "Fortunately there was no snow, and although it was very cold I lay down on the ground and fell fast asleep [...] There was no moon, and the darkness of the night was increased by a thick fog which made marching very difficult."
- **grade**: B: participant's testimony on the day, but written long after (memoir composed after 1815, published 1891).
- **label**: fact (weather), but it says nothing about what was worn.
- **notes**: Marbot does not link the cold to dress. Same page: Coignet (below) says the stream "était si gelée qu'elle ne fit aucun obstacle".

- **id**: `weather.morning.fog-sun`
- **claim**: Marbot places the French centre hidden in thick fog in the Goldbach ravine, the "sun of Austerlitz" appearing as it advanced on Pratzen.
- **value (source's words)**: "caché par un épais brouillard"; "ce brillant soleil d'Austerlitz"
- **source**: marbot1891, t. I, p. 260 (leaf 284), https://archive.org/details/mmoiresdugnralba01marb/page/260/mode/1up
- **quote**: "le centre, formé par les troupes des maréchaux Soult et Bernadotte, placé par l'Empereur au fond du ravin de Goldbach où il était caché par un épais brouillard, s'élançait vers le coteau sur lequel est situé le village de Pratzen. Ce fut à ce moment que parut dans tout son éclat ce brillant soleil d'Austerlitz" (OCR) / "the centre [...] placed by the Emperor at the bottom of the Goldbach ravine where it was hidden by a thick fog, rushed toward the slope on which the village of Pratzen stands. It was at that moment that the brilliant sun of Austerlitz appeared in all its brightness"
- **grade**: B: participant's memoir, written later.
- **label**: fact
- **notes**: No link to dress. Stutterheim 1806, p. 83 independently records a sudden "brouillard très-épais" in the Telnitz valley about 9 a.m. (next block).

- **id**: `weather.telnitz.fog-9h`
- **claim**: Stutterheim (Austrian, present) records that about nine o'clock a very thick fog suddenly darkened the Telnitz valley and caused confusion in the Allied first column.
- **value (source's words)**: "un brouillard très-épais, qui tout-à-coup obscurcit cette vallée"
- **source**: stutterheim1806, pp. 83-84 (leaves n86-n87), https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_bLCe2qyA_D8C/page/n86/mode/1up
- **quote**: "les Français, profitant alors d'un brouillard très-épais, qui tout-à-coup obscurcit cette vallée, s'emparèrent de nouveau du village [...] le régiment russe de New-Ingermannland devoit les soutenir, et fit une retraite qui, au milieu de ce brouillard, mit la confusion dans une partie de la colonne." (p. 83 read on the page image, https://archive.org/download/bub_gb_bLCe2qyA_D8C/page/n86.jpg; the p. 84 words "fit" and "mit" are from the OCR, which reads "fil" and "mil", not checked on the image) / "the French, taking advantage of a very thick fog that suddenly darkened this valley, took the village again [...] the Russian regiment of New Ingermanland was to support them, and made a retreat which, in the middle of this fog, threw part of the column into confusion."
- **grade**: B: relation by a participant printed in 1806 (near-contemporary, but not dated 1804-1805, so B by the brief's rule).
- **label**: fact
- **notes**: No statement about dress.

- **id**: `weather.ponds.frozen`
- **claim**: The ponds and the Goldbach were frozen on the day (ice thick enough for troops to try to cross, not strong enough to hold all).
- **value (source's words)**: "le lac qui étoit gelé, mais pas assez"; "si gelée qu'elle ne fit aucun obstacle"; "la glace des étangs. Elle était fort épaisse"
- **source**: stutterheim1806, p. 118; coignet1909, p. 120 (leaf 154); marbot1891, t. I, p. 263
- **quote**: Stutterheim: "Beaucoup d'entre ceux qui étoient en déroute se jetèrent sur le lac qui étoit gelé, mais pas assez cependant pour que quelques-uns n'y périssent." (OCR) / "Many of those in rout threw themselves on the lake, which was frozen, but not enough to prevent some perishing there." Coignet: "Devant nous, une rivière à franchir, mais elle était si gelée qu'elle ne fit aucun obstacle." (OCR) / "In front of us a river to cross, but it was so frozen that it was no obstacle." Marbot: "le plus grand nombre des ennemis, principalement les Russes, cherchent un passage sur la glace des étangs. Elle était fort épaisse" (OCR) / "most of the enemy, mainly the Russians, seek a way over the ice of the ponds. It was very thick".
- **grade**: B for all three (Stutterheim printed 1806; Coignet and Marbot memoirs written later).
- **label**: fact (frozen); disputed in degree (Stutterheim "pas assez" vs Marbot "fort épaisse"); outside this file's dress topic except as context for cold.
- **notes**: Coignet's passage concerns the days before the battle (the Guard's approach to its camp). None of the three links the cold to greatcoats.

### Dress and headgear actually seen on the day

- **id**: `fr.guard.foot-grenadiers.bearskin-on-day`
- **claim**: A grenadier of the Guard describes the Guard foot (with Oudinot's grenadiers) as "bearskins" on the morning of 2 December, i.e. the bearskin cap was worn in battle, not stowed.
- **value (source's words)**: "bonnets à poils"
- **source**: coignet1909, p. 122 (leaf 156), https://archive.org/details/lescahiersducapi00coig/page/122/mode/1up
- **quote**: "Lorsque les colonnes furent passées, l'Empereur nous fit suivre le mouvement. Nous étions vingt-cinq mille bonnets à poils, et des gaillards." (OCR) / "When the columns had gone by, the Emperor had us follow the movement. We were twenty-five thousand bearskins, and fine fellows."
- **grade**: B: participant's memoir written decades later, not dated 1805.
- **label**: uncertain (one later memoir; the figure "vingt-cinq mille" is an evident exaggeration, so the phrase is a figure of speech for the Guard and Oudinot's grenadiers)
- **notes**: It does not say whether the bearskins were covered or whether greatcoats were worn. The Guard crossed Vienna "en grande tenue" by order (p. 120: "il donna ses ordres pour que sa garde se mît en grande tenue"), which concerns the entry into Vienna in November, not the battle.

### Colours and standards carried on the day

- **id**: `fr.4e-ligne.eagle-carried-and-lost`
- **claim**: A battalion of the French 4e de ligne carried its eagle into the fight on the Pratzen and lost it to the Russian Guard cavalry; Stutterheim (1806) names the Life Guard Horse ("gardes à cheval du Grand-Duc"), Marbot the Chevalier Guards and "the cuirassiers of Grand Duke Constantine".
- **value (source's words)**: "une aigle française d'un bataillon du quatrième régiment"; "lui enlevèrent son aigle"; "la seule aigle que nous ayons perdue"
- **source**: stutterheim1806, p. 107 (leaf n110, read on the page image https://archive.org/download/bub_gb_bLCe2qyA_D8C/page/n110.jpg); marbot1891, t. I, pp. 260 and 264 (leaves 284, 288); coignet1909, p. 122 ("un bataillon du 4e", the eagle not named)
- **quote**: Stutterheim: "Le régiment des gardes à cheval du Grand-Duc, pour dégager l'infanterie, fit une charge sur le flanc des Français, arrêta sur ce point la cavalerie, la renversa et attaqua l'infanterie ennemie [...]. Le régiment des gardes à cheval prit à cette occasion une aigle française d'un bataillon du quatrième régiment." (OCR) / "The Grand Duke's regiment of Horse Guards, to relieve the infantry, charged the French flank, stopped the cavalry there, overthrew it and attacked the enemy infantry [...]. On this occasion the Horse Guards regiment took a French eagle from a battalion of the fourth regiment." Marbot p. 260: "un bataillon du 4e de ligne [...] fut chargé et enfoncé par les chevaliers-gardes et les cuirassiers du grand-duc Constantin, frère d'Alexandre, qui lui enlevèrent son aigle!..." (OCR) / "a battalion of the 4th of the line [...] was charged and broken by the Chevalier Guards and the cuirassiers of Grand Duke Constantine, Alexander's brother, who took its eagle from it!" Marbot p. 264: "la seule aigle que nous ayons perdue appartînt au 4e de ligne" (OCR) / "the only eagle we lost belonged to the 4th of the line".
- **grade**: B: Stutterheim's relation printed 1806 (near-contemporary, B by the brief's rule) with Marbot and Savary agreeing; Marbot's details B.
- **label**: fact (a battalion of the 4e carried an eagle on the day and lost it); disputed (which Russian Guard regiment took it).
- **notes**: Bears on "who carried colours": at least one French line battalion carried its eagle in action. It does not show whether every battalion carried one.

- **id**: `ru.guard.standards-taken`
- **claim**: Marbot (present) says the French Guard cavalry took many Russian standards in the charge on the Pratzen, and that Gérard's painting shows a dying chasseur and the mameluke Mustapha each presenting one; Coignet tells of a mameluke bringing back a Russian standard three times.
- **value (source's words)**: "beaucoup d'étendards"; "l'étendard qu'il venait de prendre"; "un drapeau ennemi"; "un étendard russe"
- **source**: marbot1891, t. I, pp. 260-262; coignet1909, p. 335 (leaf 369, "Variante du récit de la bataille d'Austerlitz")
- **quote**: Marbot p. 260-261: "Nos cavaliers enlevèrent beaucoup d'étendards et de prisonniers, parmi lesquels se trouvait le prince Repnin, commandant des chevaliers-gardes." (OCR) / "Our horsemen took many standards and prisoners, among them Prince Repnin, commander of the Chevalier Guards." Coignet p. 335: "L'un d'eux revint à trois reprises différentes apporter à l'Empereur un étendard russe ; à la troisième l'Empereur voulut le retenir, mais il s'élança de nouveau, et ne revint plus." (OCR) / "One of them [the mamelukes] came back three separate times to bring the Emperor a Russian standard; at the third the Emperor wanted to keep him, but he dashed off again and did not return."
- **grade**: B: participants' memoirs written later.
- **label**: fact (Russian Guard cavalry standards were carried in action and some were taken); uncertain on numbers.
- **notes**: Neither describes the standards' appearance. Marbot also vouches for Gérard's painting (1810): "J'étais présent à cette scène imposante, que ce peintre a reproduite avec une exactitude remarquable. Toutes les têtes sont des portraits" (p. 261, OCR) / "I was present at that imposing scene, which this painter reproduced with remarkable accuracy. All the heads are portraits". This supports the painting as a record of persons, not necessarily of dress.

### Horses

Then the Emperor let loose the black horses, that is, the horse grenadiers, commanded by General Bessières."
- **grade**: B proposed (participant's testimony about the day, but written decades later, an "addition" from the manuscript printed by Larchey); C if the coordinator treats a memoir written so long after as only "later evidence".

- **id**: `weather.night1to2.clear-cold`
- **claim**: Thiébault (St-Hilaire's division) describes the small hours of 2 December as a clear and extremely cold night, the fog lying in the valleys at daybreak.

- **notes**: No link to dress.

- **value (source's words)**: "les sacs de nos soldats"

- **label**: uncertain (one memoir; the phrase is figurative, "pour ainsi dire", but presupposes packs worn)
