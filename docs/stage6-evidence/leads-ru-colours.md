# Russian colours (знамёна) and standards (штандарты), 2 December 1805: evidence dossier

Prepared 4 October 2026 for the Austerlitz command map: open question 1 ("the pattern of Russian infantry flags") and
decision 36 (the provisional standard ratio 1.6, `docs/STAGE2_SPEC.md` §I.1). This is research only. No project file was changed.

## 0. Read this first: what this file is, and what it is not

**Access.** The environment's egress proxy blocked every research host tried (list in §10.3). Web search worked until the
session's shared budget of 200 searches ran out; 29 of those searches were made for this brief. GitHub was reachable. As a result:

- **Excluded:** three texts were reached through OCR copies held in other GitHub repositories (§10.1, R1-R3). This session may
  read only its own repository, so those readings are **not used**; the rows that rested on them are marked EXCLUDED and ungraded.
- Every other row rests on a **search-tool extract**: a machine-written summary of a result page, not the page itself. The
  brief forbids citing such text, so these rows are **leads, not evidence**. Each row names the page or book the extract
  points to, is labelled *uncertain (unverified lead)*, and must be read in the named source before any use. Extract wording
  is given only to help find the passage. It is not a quotation of the source and may be paraphrased or wrong. Several extracts
  may also go back to one compilation (vexillographia.ru), so agreement between them does not prove independent sources.
- This follows the project's own precedent for blocked sources (`docs/STAGE2_SPEC.md` §I.2, the meres note: "every figure
  must be read from the source itself before any use").

**Grades** (A: documented for 1805; B: documented for the period and probable for 1805; C: reconstructed or disputed) are the
grade a claim would carry *if the named source confirms it*, written "B (prov.)". No row carries an unqualified grade.

**Labels**: fact, disputed, derived, inference, uncertain, design decision.

**Units** (derived conversions): 1 arshin (аршин) = 71.12 cm; 1 vershok (вершок) = 1/16 arshin = 4.445 cm. These are the
legal values of 1835 and were not checked for 1797.

**Bottom line for the open question.** It stays open. The leads point the same way: in 1805 most line regiments probably still
carried Paul's 1797 pattern, some had the 1800 pattern and some the new pattern of 10 June 1803; there were two colours per
battalion from 1802 and one white colour per regiment; and the cloth was about 142 cm square. **None of this is verified.** The
details the brief asks about (the corner "flames", where the Maltese cross went, the corner devices, which battalion carried the
white colour) are **not established** by anything gathered here.

## 8. Disagreements

1. **How many colours were taken.** The proclamation claims "quarante drapeaux" plus the Russian Guard's standards (6.1); the
   Brünn letter claims 45 (6.2), which would agree if the 45 includes the Guard standards. Russian-side reckonings give 29-30
   Russian colours (6.4, 6.5a; the Notre-Dame protocol counts bare poles too). A count from French regimental histories gives
   "no more than 25" (6.5b). The project's lede (`data.js`, "about 180 guns and 45 standards") follows Napoleon's figure. The
   Russian specialist figures are lower and the matter is disputed. This note proposes no data change.
2. **Dates and where the colours went.** The Brünn letter is dated 9 or 11 Dec 1805. The Notre-Dame reception is given as 1 Jan
   1806 or as a protocol of 19 Jan 1806. One extract says the colours went on to the Invalides after a month in Notre-Dame.
3. **The finial.** A crowned eagle with sceptre and orb (1797, 5.5) against Paul's cypher under one or two crowns (1800, 1.11).
4. **The Azov regiment.** The extract says "all four colours lost, none to the enemy" (6.6), which is self-contradictory as summarised.
   (A second account was reached only through an excluded copy and is not used.)
5. (Withdrawn: it compared two excluded copies.)
6. **Cloth size.** 2 arshins (142.2 cm) and 1.43 m agree. The "12 vershoks (53 cm)" in the same extract belongs, by inference,
   to standards.
7. **Hussar standards.** The brief assumes them. The extract in 4.1 says that from 1797 only cuirassiers and dragoons had standards.
