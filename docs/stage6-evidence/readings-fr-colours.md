# Stage 6B research: French colours, eagles and standards at Austerlitz (2 December 1805)

Topic: the colours, eagles and standards carried by the French army on 2 December 1805, and the measurements needed to draw them to
scale (cloth, staff, eagle; a sourced stature for the ratio, owner decision 106). Agent: fr-colours. Written 5 October 2026,
progressively. Scope: the 1804 model (pattern, inscriptions, size, fringe, cravate, staff, eagle); how many were carried (per battalion
/ squadron in 1805; light infantry; cavalry by arm; the Guard; artillery; Oudinot's grenadiers); the 4e de ligne's lost eagle; staff
height and soldier stature; the Italian Royal Guard's colours. Every claim below was read in the source in this session unless marked
NOT FOUND. Reading list: `docs/stage6-evidence/leads-fr-colours.md` (leads only).

## Claims

- **id**: `fr.flag.arrete-2-thermidor-XII`
- **claim**: An arrêté of 2 thermidor an XII (21 July 1804), signed Berthier, as quoted by Bouillé, ordered the an XI flags kept with "Empire Français" for "République Française" on one face, and on the other a blue disc in a gold glory, inscribed "Napoléon, Empereur des Français (à tel corps)", the imperial eagle above, "Valeur et discipline" below; colours and cut unchanged for each arm.
- **value (source's words)**: "un disque d'azur au centre d'une gloire ou rayons d'or"; "Napoléon, Empereur des Français (à tel corps)"; "Valeur et discipline"
- **source**: bouille1875, p. 207 (leaf n248), https://archive.org/details/lesdrapeauxfran00boui/page/n248
- **quote**: « On laissera d'un côté les trophées d'armes analogues à chaque corps, en substituant aux mots République Française ceux de Empire Français ; sur l'autre côté on substituera à ce qui existe un disque d'azur au centre d'une gloire ou rayons d'or et entouré aux trois quarts par deux branches de laurier. Le disque portera Napoléon, Empereur des Français (à tel corps) ; au-dessus sera l'aigle impérial en or appuyé sur une foudre » … « Arrêté à Paris le 2 thermidor an XII. Signé : Maréchal Berthier. » (read on the page image). = "On one face the trophies of arms proper to each corps will be kept, with the words Empire Français for République Française; on the other face a blue disc in a gold glory, three-quarters surrounded by two laurel branches. The disc will bear Napoléon, Emperor of the French (to such corps); above it the imperial eagle in gold resting on a thunderbolt … Decided at Paris, 2 thermidor an XII. Signed: Marshal Berthier."
- **grade**: C, a dated 1804 text, but read only as quoted in an 1875 work, and contradicted by the pattern actually documented for the flags carried (see `fr.flag.pattern-1804` and Disagreements); not shown to have been executed.
- **label**: disputed
- **notes**: Bouillé adds "Ce modèle est resté généralement en usage pendant la durée de l'Empire" (p. 207), yet his own plate XIV shows the lozenge flag with "L'EMPEREUR DES FRANÇAIS AU 126ème RÉGt DE LIGNE", not this design. Regnault 1967 (pp. 19-20 and plate, `fr.eagle.regnault-berthier-model-rejected`) states that this model of 21 July 1804 was rejected by the Emperor: not the 1805 flag.

- **id**: `fr.flag.anXI-per-battalion`
- **claim**: The infantry flags approved in an XI (1803) were inscribed with the battalion number ("me bataillon"), which Bouillé reads as showing that each battalion still had its own flag.
- **value (source's words)**: "me bataillon"; "alors chaque bataillon avait encore son drapeau"
- **source**: bouille1875, p. 204 (leaf n243), https://archive.org/details/lesdrapeauxfran00boui/page/n243 (text read in the archive.org OCR)
- **quote**: « au-dessous du trophée sur un ruban semblable me bataillon, le numéro du régiment aux quatre angles du drapeau. (On voit par cette description qu'alors chaque bataillon avait encore son drapeau.) » (OCR) = "below the trophy, on a similar ribbon, nth battalion, the regiment's number in the four corners of the flag. (One sees from this description that each battalion then still had its flag.)"
- **grade**: B, specialist (1875) describing the approved models of an XI held at the Dépôt de la guerre; 1803, before the eagles.
- **label**: fact (for an XI); inference (Bouillé's) for one flag per battalion
- **notes**: The same page gives "Modèle approuvé le 26 ventôse an XI" for line infantry; light infantry and artillery models also carry "me bataillon" (p. 205).

- **id**: `fr.flag.pattern-1804`
- **claim**: Bouillé's plate of the line infantry flag "sous Napoléon Ier" shows a white lozenge touching the four edges, the corner triangles blue at the upper hoist and lower fly and red at the upper fly and lower hoist, the regimental number in a laurel wreath in each corner, a laurel frieze along the lozenge's edges, the inscription "L'EMPEREUR DES FRANÇAIS AU 126ème RÉGt DE LIGNE", a gilt eagle on the staff and a blue-white-red cravate with fringed ends; no fringe on the cloth.
- **value (source's words)**: plate caption "Infanterie de ligne sous Napoléon Ier"; inscription "L'EMPEREUR DES FRANÇAIS AU 126ème RÉGT DE LIGNE"
- **source**: bouille1875, pl. XIV ("Page 206"), leaf n246, https://archive.org/details/lesdrapeauxfran00boui/page/n246
- **quote**: (image; caption and inscription as printed) "Infanterie de ligne sous Napoléon 1er" / "L'EMPEREUR DES FRANÇAIS AU 126ème RÉGT DE LIGNE"
- **grade**: C for 1805: a chromolithograph of 1875 of the flag of a regiment raised after 1805 (the 126e), not an object of 1805; supports the type only.
- **label**: uncertain (one later image) until confirmed by a dated object or text
- **notes**: The colours of the corners as printed: upper hoist blue, upper fly red, lower hoist red, lower fly blue (obverse as drawn, staff at the left). The same plate shows a second "Drapeau d'Infanterie de ligne (Empire)", a vertical tricolour with AUSTERLITZ IENA EILAU EKMUHL WAGRAM: the 1812-type, not 1805.

- **id**: `fr.eagle.carried-without-cloth`
- **claim**: Bouillé states that the eagle was usually carried in battle while the cloth stayed with the regimental chest at the colonel's quarters, citing an officer of the 59e in Spain (1808-1814); and that before doubtful actions the eagle was left behind, general after the retreat from Russia.
- **value (source's words)**: "L'aigle était d'habitude portée dans les combats et l'étoffe restait avec la caisse du régiment au logement du colonel"
- **source**: bouille1875, p. 208, leaf n249, https://archive.org/details/lesdrapeauxfran00boui/page/n249
- **quote**: « L'aigle était d'habitude portée dans les combats et l'étoffe restait avec la caisse du régiment au logement du colonel. Des années entières se passaient sans que le drapeau parût devant les troupes. » (read on the page image) = "The eagle was usually carried in battle and the cloth stayed with the regiment's chest at the colonel's quarters. Whole years passed without the flag appearing before the troops."
- **grade**: C for 1805: an 1875 generalisation whose only witness is an officer of the Peninsular War (1808-1814).
- **label**: uncertain
- **notes**: Not evidence for Austerlitz either way; contradicted by Hollander 1902 (`fr.flag.carried-with-cloth`). The 1805 texts on the 4e's loss speak of "son aigle" and "le porte-drapeau" (Corr. 9610) and Debidour of "le drapeau" and "son aigle" interchangeably: not decisive.

- **id**: `fr.eagle.order-27-july-1804`
- **claim**: On 27 July 1804 Napoleon had Berthier told that the new flags should differ from the existing ones: the spread eagle of the Empire's seal on top of the staff "as the Romans carried it", the cloth below it, much smaller than the present flags (reducible by half), tricolour, inscribed "L'Empereur des Français à tel régiment"; the eagle to constitute the flag essentially, the cloth replaceable; the eagle to be both solid and light; a model to be made.
- **value (source's words)**: "L'aigle éployée … sera placée sur la sommité du bâton du drapeau"; "beaucoup moins d'étendue que les drapeaux actuels"; "de trois couleurs"; "L'Empereur des Français à tel régiment"; "rendre l'aigle tout à la fois solide et légère"
- **source**: corr9_1858, no. 7876, "Au maréchal Berthier", Pont-de-Briques, 8 thermidor an XII (27 juillet 1804), pp. 434-435 (leaves n443-n444), https://archive.org/details/correspondancede09napouoft/page/n443
- **quote**: « L'aigle éployée, telle qu'elle se trouvera sur le sceau de l'Empire, sera placée sur la sommité du bâton du drapeau, de la même manière que la portaient les Romains. On attacherait au-dessous le drapeau, à la distance où se trouvait le labarum. Il aurait beaucoup moins d'étendue que les drapeaux actuels, qui sont très-embarrassants, et serait de trois couleurs, comme ceux-ci. L'étendue du drapeau pourrait ainsi être réduite à moitié. On y lirait ces mots : L'Empereur des Français à tel régiment. » (read on the page images) = "The spread eagle, as it will appear on the seal of the Empire, will be placed at the top of the flag's staff, as the Romans carried it. The flag would be attached below, at the distance where the labarum was. It would be much smaller than the present flags, which are very cumbersome, and would be of three colours like them. The flag's size could thus be reduced by half. On it would be read: The Emperor of the French to such regiment."
- **grade**: A, a dated primary text of July 1804 (the intention; the executed model must be shown by objects or later texts).
- **label**: fact (that this was ordered as a design brief); the final dimensions are not given here
- **notes**: This is the source of the inscription formula found on the surviving pattern (see `fr.flag.pattern-1804`) and contradicts the disc design of the arrêté quoted by Bouillé (`fr.flag.arrete-2-thermidor-XII`), dated 2 thermidor an XII (21 July 1804), six days earlier; Regnault 1967 dates Berthier's arrêté to 21 July 1804 and treats this letter as the Emperor's response to it.

- **id**: `fr.eagle.distribution-5-dec-1804`
- **claim**: The eagles were distributed at the Champ-de-Mars, Paris, on 14 frimaire an XIII (5 December 1804), Napoleon's words beginning "Soldats, voilà vos drapeaux; ces aigles vous serviront toujours de point de ralliement".
- **value (source's words)**: "Distribution des aigles au Champ-de-Mars"; "voilà vos drapeaux; ces aigles"
- **source**: corr10_1858, no. 8203, "Paroles de l'Empereur lors de la distribution des aigles au Champ-de-Mars", Paris, 14 frimaire an XIII (5 décembre 1804), p. 61 (leaf n70; "Extrait du Moniteur"), https://archive.org/details/correspondancede10napouoft/page/n70
- **quote**: « Soldats, voilà vos drapeaux; ces aigles vous serviront toujours de point de ralliement; ils seront partout où votre Empereur les jugera nécessaires pour la défense de son trône et de son peuple. » (read on the page image) = "Soldiers, here are your flags; these eagles will always be your rallying point; they will be wherever your Emperor judges them necessary for the defence of his throne and his people."
- **grade**: A, dated primary text (Moniteur, December 1804).
- **label**: fact
- **notes**: Gives the date only; nothing on numbers per unit.

- **id**: `fr.eagle.4e-ligne-1st-battalion`
- **claim**: The eagle lost at Austerlitz was that of the 1st battalion of the 4e de ligne: at the review of Vandamme's division ("Mardi", as reported in the order of the day of 25 December 1805) its "porte-drapeau" was reported killed in a charge in the thickest of the mêlée, the loss unnoticed in the smoke; the battalion offered two flags it had taken from two Russian battalions and was promised a new eagle.
- **value (source's words)**: "1er bataillon du 4e régiment de ligne, qui avait été entamé à la bataille d'Austerlitz et y avait perdu son aigle"; "le porte-drapeau ayant été tué dans une charge"
- **source**: corr11_1858, no. 9610, Ordre du jour, Quartier impérial, Schœnbrunn, 4 nivôse an XIV (25 décembre 1805), pp. 499-500 (leaves n508-n509), https://archive.org/details/correspondancede11napouoft/page/n508
- **quote**: « Arrivé au 1er bataillon du 4e régiment de ligne, qui avait été entamé à la bataille d'Austerlitz et y avait perdu son aigle, l'Empereur lui dit : "Soldats, qu'avez-vous fait de l'aigle que je vous avais donnée?" … Le major a répondu que, le porte-drapeau ayant été tué dans une charge au moment de la plus forte mêlée, personne ne s'en était aperçu au milieu de la fumée » (read on the page image) = "Arriving at the 1st battalion of the 4th line regiment, which had been mauled at the battle of Austerlitz and had lost its eagle there, the Emperor said to it: 'Soldiers, what have you done with the eagle I gave you?' … The major replied that, the colour-bearer having been killed in a charge at the height of the mêlée, nobody had noticed it in the smoke".
- **grade**: A, an order of the day of 25 December 1805 about the battle.
- **label**: fact
- **notes**: The eagle is "son aigle", the battalion's own: consistent with one eagle per battalion in the 4e de ligne on 2 December 1805 (inference: a battalion-specific loss). The bearer is called "porte-drapeau" (no rank given). The same text says the two flags taken were from "deux bataillons russes"; Fraser 1912 (pp. 112, 117) says "two Austrian standards": a disagreement (Disagreements table). The 30th Bulletin (next claim) does not mention the eagle.

- **id**: `fr.eagle.4e-ligne-bulletin`
- **claim**: The 30th Bulletin of the Grande Armée (3 December 1805) reports only that a battalion of the 4e de ligne was charged and overthrown by the Russian Imperial Guard cavalry, and claims the Russian Guard's standards taken.
- **value (source's words)**: "Un bataillon du 4e de ligne fut chargé par la garde impériale russe à cheval, et culbuté"
- **source**: corr11_1858, no. 9541, 30e Bulletin de la Grande Armée, Austerlitz, 12 frimaire an XIV (3 décembre 1805), p. 451 (leaf n460) to p. 452, https://archive.org/details/correspondancede11napouoft/page/n460
- **quote**: « Un bataillon du 4e de ligne fut chargé par la garde impériale russe à cheval, et culbuté; mais l'Empereur n'était pas loin » … « dans un moment la garde russe fut en déroute; colonel, artillerie, étendards, tout fut enlevé. » (OCR, the first words checked on the page image) = "A battalion of the 4th of the line was charged by the Russian Imperial Guard horse and overthrown; but the Emperor was not far away … in a moment the Russian Guard was routed; colonel, artillery, standards, all were taken."
- **grade**: A, dated primary text (propaganda; silent on the eagle).
- **label**: fact (what the Bulletin says)
- **notes**: Silence on the eagle; the order of the day of 25 December 1805 supplies the loss.

- **id**: `fr.eagle.fraser-dimensions`
- **claim**: Fraser (1912) gives for the eagle: bird 8 in. from head to feet, 9.5 in. across the wings, on a brass tablet 3 in. square with the regiment's number, gilt copper, whole weight 3.5 lb; an oak staff 8 ft long painted "bleu impérial"; the silk flag 35 in. along the staff and 33 in. in the fly.
- **value (source's words)**: "eight inches from head to feet"; "nine and a half inches"; "three inches square"; "three and a half pounds"; "a stout oaken staff … eight feet long and painted bleu impérial"; "thirty-five inches along the staff and thirty-three lengthwise, in the fly"
- **source**: fraser1912, p. 11, footnote 1 (leaf n36), https://archive.org/details/wardramaofeagles00fras/page/n36
- **quote**: "the bird measuring eight inches from head to feet, and in the spread of its wings from tip to tip, nine and a half inches. Below the thunderbolt, as base and support, was a tablet of brass, three inches square … The weight of the whole … was just three and a half pounds avoirdupois, and a stout oaken staff was provided, eight feet long and painted bleu impérial, to which the silken regimental colour was attached; the flag being thirty-five inches along the staff and thirty-three lengthwise, in the fly." (read on the page image, p. 11 footnote 1, leaf n36; "bleu impérial" in italics in print)
- **grade**: C, a 1912 popular history that gives no source for the figures.
- **label**: uncertain
- **notes**: In metric (derived): bird 20.3 cm high, 24.1 cm across; tablet 7.6 cm; weight 1.59 kg; staff 2.44 m; cloth 88.9 x 83.8 cm.
