# Stage 6B research: Russian colours (знамёна) and standards (штандарты), 2 December 1805

- Topic: the colours and standards carried by the Russian army at Austerlitz on 2 December 1805; their patterns, numbers, carriers,
  dimensions (cloth, staff, finial); cavalry standards; colours lost at Austerlitz; a sourced stature for the ratio question (decision 106).
- Date of this research: 5 October 2026 (research agent, Stage 6B).
- Scope: only what was read in this session in the source itself; everything else is "NOT FOUND". Reading list:
  `docs/stage6-evidence/leads-ru-colours.md` (leads, unverified; its EXCLUDED rows not used).
- Status: complete for this session (5 Oct 2026).

## Key findings (summary; each point is a claim below)

1. **What the line regiments carried in 1805 is documented, regiment by regiment, as an inference from read rules**: Paul I made colours permanent (`ru.col.term`); the order of 21 March 1802 (PSZ no. 20.193, read) told every regiment to keep two colours per battalion *of those it already held* and send the rest to arsenals (`ru.col.1802.psz`); the 1803 designs were "for future grants" (`ru.col.1803.future`). So the Austerlitz regiments most probably carried their Paul-era colours (1797-1799 pattern; the 1800 pattern for Arkhangelogorod and Smolensk), two per battalion present, the white one in the 1st battalion (Viskovatov); the Galicia regiment (formed 1803) the 1803 design. Table in section A2.
2. **The 1797-1799 pattern** (Viskovatov 1850, from surviving colours): square cloth 2 × 2 arshins (142 cm), cross and four corners drawn as radiating wedges round a light-orange circle with a black eagle in green laurels tied with blue; the white colour (one per regiment) a white cross with coloured corners, the others coloured crosses; schemes assigned regiment by regiment; staff 4½ arshins (320 cm), flat gilt spearhead with the eagle 5½ vershoks (24 cm), gilt butt 2 vershoks; staffs pale yellow, white, coffee or black by regiment.
3. **Cavalry**: cuirassier standards 10 × 12 vershoks (44 × 53 cm), dragoon standards 12 × 12 vershoks (53 cm), one per squadron (five), the chef's squadron's white; staffs green with gold stripes. No hussar standards are listed for 1797-1801 (silence only); the Pavlograd Hussars received St George standards in 1807.
4. **Jägers carried no colours** (1801 state; only by special grant).
5. **Guard**: disputed whether the Guard infantry carried the 1800 or the 1799 (Maltese-cross) colours (Viskovatov contradicts himself); the Chevalier Guard's standards' cloths are unknown (Viskovatov) or crimson with a white cross (1832 regimental history); the Life Guard Horse's last pre-1806 issue is of 1799.
6. **Losses**: Napoleon claimed 40, then 45 colours (both armies) and "the standards of the Russian imperial guard"; Sokolov 2006 reports 29 Russian by French data and Andolenko's 14-17 complete colours (Podolsk 5, Azov 3, Kursk 2-5, Narva 2, Arkhangelogorod 1, Perm 1); Mikhailovsky-Danilevsky records cloths torn off and saved (Perm, Azov, Narva).
7. **Stature**: the statutory minimum for recruits was 2 arshins 4 vershoks (160.0 cm), in the 1804 instruction (PSZ no. 21.490, read); the derived pole-top ratio for a grounded 1797 staff is 2.0-2.2 times that minimum (`ru.ratio.derived`).

### A. Infantry colours: the Paul I pattern of 1797-1799 (Viskovatov, part 9)

- **id**: `ru.col.term`
- **claim**: Paul I made colours and standards serve without a fixed term (before him they were issued for set periods like other equipment); this is why colours issued in 1797-1800 can still be expected in service in 1805.
- **value (source's words)**: «служили безсрочно» (served without term)
- **source**: viskovatov9_1850, p. 68; https://archive.org/details/viskovatovaleksandrvasilevich180418550/page/n77
- **quote**: «До восшествія на Престолъ Императора Павла I-го, знамена и штандарты полагались въ числѣ амуничныхъ и оружейныхъ вещей и на опредѣленные сроки. Императоръ Павелъ I назначилъ, чтобы, какъ знамена, такъ и штандарты, служили безсрочно» — "Until the accession of Emperor Paul I, colours and standards were counted among the equipment and weapons and [issued] for fixed terms. Emperor Paul I ordained that both colours and standards should serve without term."
- **grade**: B — specialist work (1850) from the regulations; the measure dates from 1796-1801 and is not itself shown to be in force on 2 Dec 1805 by this passage (but see `ru.col.1801`, where Viskovatov describes the colours Alexander I found at his accession, and `ru.col.1803.future`).
- **label**: fact
- **notes**: The inference that Paul's colours were still carried in 1805 is drawn in `ru.col.1805.which` and labelled inference.

- **id**: `ru.col.1797.form`
- **claim**: All colours issued to grenadier, musketeer and garrison regiments in 1797, 1798 and 1799 were alike in form and size: a cross, four corners and a circle, 2 arshins long and 2 arshins wide (142.2 cm square, derived), of silk, preferably gros de Tours, sometimes camlet.
- **value (source's words)**: «изъ креста, четырехъ угловъ и круга»; «по 2 аршина»; «гродетуръ», «камлотъ»
- **source**: viskovatov9_1850, pp. 68-69; https://archive.org/details/viskovatovaleksandrvasilevich180418550/page/n77 and /page/n78
- **quote**: «Всѣ знамена, жалованныя Гренадерскимъ, Мушкетерскимъ и Гарнизоннымъ полкамъ, въ продолженіе 1797, 1798 и 1799 годовъ, были одинаковы между собою, какъ формою, такъ и величиною, состоя изъ креста, четырехъ угловъ и круга, и имѣя, какъ въ-длину, такъ и въ-ширину, по 2 аршина, а дѣлались изъ шелковыхъ матерій, предпочтительно изъ гродетура. Иногда употреблялся и камлотъ.» — "All colours granted to grenadier, musketeer and garrison regiments during 1797, 1798 and 1799 were alike in form and size, consisting of a cross, four corners and a circle, being 2 arshins both in length and in width, and were made of silk stuffs, preferably gros de Tours. Sometimes camlet was used too."
- **grade**: B — specialist work describing the 1797-1799 issues from the regulations; for 1805 see `ru.col.1805.which`.
- **label**: fact (dimension in cm: derived)
- **notes**: Square cloth of 2 × 2 arshins = 142.24 cm (derived). Agrees with the leads' 2 arshins/1.43 m (leads 5.1, 5.2), now read in the source. **Basis of the dimensions** (Viskovatov's own note 141, p. XII of the notes, image checked, https://archive.org/details/viskovatovaleksandrvasilevich180418550/page/n153): «Особыхъ правилъ для постройки знаменъ не было, или, по крайней мѣрѣ, не отыскано, а все сказанное здѣсь о ихъ величинѣ и видѣ, основано на знаменахъ времени Императора Павла I, сохранившихся донынѣ, какъ въ рисункахъ, такъ и въ самой натурѣ.» ("There were no special rules for making colours, or at least none were found; all that is said here about their size and appearance is based on colours of Emperor Paul I's time preserved to this day, both in drawings and in the objects themselves.") So the sizes are measurements of surviving colours reported by Viskovatov, not a regulation's figures. The per-regiment entries cite, note by note, the imperial charter (Высочайшая грамота) of each issue and Prince Dolgorukov's «Хроника Россійской Арміи» with its drawings of colours in His Majesty's Own Library (notes 142ff).

- **id**: `ru.col.1797.staff`
- **claim**: The 1797-1799 infantry colour's staff was 4½ arshins long (320 cm, derived); its finial a flat gilt copper spearhead with a socket (tube), 5½ vershoks long with the tube (24.4 cm, derived), bearing a gilt double-headed eagle in its middle; a gilt copper butt (подтокъ) 2 vershoks high (8.9 cm, derived); two silver tassels with black and orange silk on a doubled silver braid, each half with its tassel 1 arshin 3 vershoks (84.5 cm, derived); the cloth's spare edge (запасъ) wrapped round the staff and nailed with gilt-headed copper nails.
- **value (source's words)**: «Длина древка была 4½ аршина; длина копья, съ трубкою — 5½ вершковъ»; «вышина подтока — 2 вершка»
- **source**: viskovatov9_1850, p. 69 (figs. 1224, 1225 in the plate volume, not seen); https://archive.org/details/viskovatovaleksandrvasilevich180418550/page/n78
- **quote**: «Къ низу древка утверждался мѣдный, вызолоченный подтокъ; на верхній конецъ насаживалось мѣдное же, вызолоченное, плоское копье, съ трубкою, имѣвшее, въ срединѣ, вызолоченное изображеніе двуглаваго орла (Рис. № 1225). Ниже копья, у края трубки, привѣшивались двѣ кисти, изъ серебра, съ чернымъ и оранжевымъ шелкомъ [...]. Длина древка была 4½ аршина; длина копья, съ трубкою — 5½ вершковъ; длина каждой половины серебряной тесьмы, съ кистью — 1 аршинъ и 3 вершка; вышина подтока — 2 вершка.» — "At the foot of the staff was fixed a gilt copper butt; on the upper end was set a flat spearhead, also of gilt copper, with a socket, having in its middle a gilt image of a double-headed eagle (fig. 1225). Below the spearhead, at the edge of the socket, hung two tassels of silver with black and orange silk [...]. The staff was 4½ arshins long; the spearhead with its socket 5½ vershoks; each half of the silver braid with its tassel 1 arshin 3 vershoks; the butt 2 vershoks high."
- **grade**: B — specialist work, for the 1797-1799 pattern; whether the staffs in use in Dec 1805 were these is inference (see `ru.col.1805.which`). The leads' 4½ arshins + 5½-vershok finial (lead 5.4, attributed there to Nicholas I) is here read for 1797-1799.
- **label**: fact (cm: derived)
- **notes**: These figures, like the cloth's, rest on surviving colours and drawings, not on a found regulation (Viskovatov's note 141, see `ru.col.1797.form`). Whether the 4½ arshins include the finial and the butt is not stated; read literally, "длина древка" is the staff, the spearhead (5½ vershoks with its socket) set on its top end and the butt on its foot. Total if the socket and butt add to the staff: 320.0 + 24.4 + 8.9 = 353.3 cm at most; if the socket sleeves the staff's top, less (derived; not resolved). Later change: on 5 Dec 1808 the staff was set at 5 arshins (see `ru.col.1808.staff`), after the period.

- **id**: `ru.col.1797.number`
- **claim**: Colours were granted to each regiment by the number of its musketeer companies; the colour of the first (the chef's) company always had a white cross with coloured corners, the other companies' colours a coloured cross.
- **value (source's words)**: «по числу состоявшихъ въ немъ Мушкетерскихъ ротъ»; «крестъ бѣлый, углы цвѣтные»; «кресты были цвѣтные»
- **source**: viskovatov9_1850, p. 70; https://archive.org/details/viskovatovaleksandrvasilevich180418550/page/n79
- **quote**: «Знамена, каждому полку, жаловались по числу состоявшихъ въ немъ Мушкетерскихъ ротъ; при чемъ знамя первой, или шефской, роты, непремѣнно имѣло крестъ бѣлый, углы цвѣтные. На знаменахъ прочихъ ротъ кресты были цвѣтные.» — "Colours were granted to each regiment according to the number of musketeer companies in it; the colour of the first, or chef's, company invariably had a white cross and coloured corners. On the colours of the other companies the crosses were coloured."
- **grade**: B — specialist work for the 1797-1799 issues; the number was changed on 21 March 1802 (see `ru.col.1802.number`).
- **label**: fact
- **notes**: The per-regiment issues listed (pp. 70-95) are almost all "10-ть знаменъ" for line grenadier and musketeer regiments (one white + nine coloured), i.e. ten companies; garrison regiments 5-20.

- **id**: `ru.col.1797.circle`
- **claim**: On all 1797-1799 colours the circle was light orange with a black double-headed eagle between two green laurel branches tied with a light-blue ribbon; the shield on the eagle's breast red (as the Moscow arms); the edge round the shield, the chain of the order of St Andrew, the crowns, sceptre and orb gold; the запас (sleeve) of the cross's colour; staffs in four colours: pale yellow (палевый), white, coffee and black.
- **value (source's words)**: «Кругъ у всѣхъ знаменъ былъ свѣтло-оранжевый, съ чернымъ, двуглавымъ орломъ»; «зелеными, лавровыми вѣтвями, связанными голубою лентою»; древки «палевый, бѣлый, кофейный и черный»
- **source**: viskovatov9_1850, p. 70 (fig. 1224); https://archive.org/details/viskovatovaleksandrvasilevich180418550/page/n79
- **quote**: «Кругъ у всѣхъ знаменъ былъ свѣтло-оранжевый, съ чернымъ, двуглавымъ орломъ и съ двумя, по сторонамъ сего послѣдняго, зелеными, лавровыми вѣтвями, связанными голубою лентою. Щитъ, на груди орла, былъ, какъ присвоено Московскому гербу, красный; каемка же, вокругъ щита, цѣпь ордена Св. Андрея Первозваннаго, короны, скипетръ и держава — золотые (Рис. № 1224). Запасъ дѣлался одного цвѣта съ крестомъ, а для древокъ существовали четыре цвѣта: палевый, бѣлый, кофейный и черный (141).» — "The circle on all colours was light orange, with a black double-headed eagle and, at its sides, two green laurel branches tied with a light-blue ribbon. The shield on the eagle's breast was red, as belongs to the Moscow arms; the edge round the shield, the chain of the order of St Andrew the First-Called, the crowns, sceptre and orb gold (fig. 1224). The sleeve was made of one colour with the cross, and for the staffs there were four colours: pale yellow, white, coffee and black."
- **grade**: B — specialist work, 1797-1799 pattern.
- **label**: fact
- **notes**: No cyphers in the corners are described for the 1797-1799 pattern (they appear in the 1800 pattern, `ru.col.1800.form`). The leads' "Moscow arms on the breast" (lead 1.4) is confirmed as a red shield; the leads' corner "flames" are not described in this text: Viskovatov describes two-colour corners only as "X съ Y пополамъ" ("X with Y, half and half"), without saying how each corner is divided (the figures in the plate volume would show it; not seen).

- **id**: `ru.col.1797.corners`
- **claim**: Regimental colour schemes were assigned regiment by regiment in 1797-1799 (not by inspection): each regiment's entry gives the white colour's corner colours, the coloured colours' cross and corners, and the staff colour; corners are often of two colours "half and half".

- **claim**: Cuirassier standards granted in 1797-1799 were of the same basis and materials as the infantry colours, rectangular, 10 vershoks along the staff by 12 vershoks long (44.5 × 53.3 cm, derived), with an embroidered soaring double-headed eagle in the lower corner nearest the staff and a cross in a glory in the opposite upper corner, Paul I's cyphers in laurels under crowns in all four corners, embroidered borders and laurel leaves between the cyphers, and a fringe on the three free edges; staffs green with gold stripes; gilt spearheads with double-headed eagles; gilt butts; silver cords and tassels with black and orange silk.
- **value (source's words)**: «четвероугольные, мѣрою: въ-ширину, по древку — 10, а въ-длину — 12 вершковъ»; «Древки штандартовъ были, какъ и прежде, зеленыя, съ золотыми полосками»
- **source**: viskovatov9_1850, pp. 102-103 (fig. 1249); https://archive.org/details/viskovatovaleksandrvasilevich180418550/page/n111 and /page/n112
- **quote**: «[Штандарты, — жалованные Кирасирскимъ полкамъ, въ 1797, 1798 и 1799 годахъ, на] такомъ же основаніи и изъ такихъ же матерій, какъ вышеописанныя, пѣхотныя знамена, — были четвероугольные, мѣрою: въ-ширину, по древку — 10, а въ-длину — 12 вершковъ. На срединѣ полотна, въ нижнемъ, къ древку ближайшемъ углу, находилось вышитое изображеніе двуглаваго, парящаго орла, а въ верхнемъ, противоположномъ предъидущему, крестъ, въ сіяніи.» — "The standards granted to cuirassier regiments in 1797, 1798 and 1799, on the same basis and of the same stuffs as the infantry colours described above, were quadrangular, measuring 10 vershoks in width along the staff and 12 vershoks in length.
