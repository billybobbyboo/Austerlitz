# Roadmap step 4: the sourcing stage's evidence

Roadmap step 4 (`docs/ROADMAP.md`; `docs/FINAL_AUDIT.md` H-6, H-17; owner decisions 125 (b), 128 (b), 130, 136). What is here:

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `readings.md` | the register of readings: every passage read for step 4, by cluster (C1 the three disputed hours; C2 the other hours; C3 Drouet, I Corps and the Guard; C4 the Zuran, the chapel, the ice and the sun; C5 the strengths and the 6B data questions; C6 the language, Blasowitz and the places), each with its source, locator, quotation, translation, grade, label, what it settles and what it does not, the map's texts it bears on and its status; the source skeptics' corrections and counter-readings; the negatives (`C<n>.n<k>`), each a search that found nothing; the sources not readable from here. Each correction of a source skeptic stands on its own line beside the researcher's value, named as the skeptic's. Generated from the research workflow's results; a later correction is made in place and dated. |
| `proposals.md` | each cluster's proposals (what its researcher proposed the data should say, on those readings) and its source skeptic's review of each: the plan's inputs, not decisions (what changed is in `CHANGELOG.md`; the choices left are the owner's questions). The registers' "proposal n" is `C<k>.p<n>` here. |
| `inventory.md`, `inventory.json` | every narrative statement of `data.js` and `analysis.js` (687), one per string, with its claim from `narrative.js`'s `STATEMENT_CLAIMS` (unchecked where it has none) and the readings it rests on; generated by `node tools/step4/inventory.js`, held current by `test.js` |

**How the readings were made.** By machine readers only: a researcher per cluster read each passage on its page image (the scan's text
layer was used to find pages; a reading made on the text layer alone says so on its quote line, one made on images with part of the passage
or its locator on the text layer says "part on the text layer" there), and a source skeptic per cluster downloaded the images again, checked
each quotation word for word, its locator, translation, grade and what it was said to settle, and looked for counter-evidence. The
exceptions are named on the status lines: the 7 text-layer readings were checked on the text layer, and the counter-readings by no other
reader. A skeptic's "CRn" in its notes numbers its own working list, not the counter-readings here (`C<k>.c<n>`): find one by the source and
page named beside it. No person has read these pages for step 4; decision 136's human second reading is an owner question of this
step.

**How the claims use them.** `narrative.js`'s `STATEMENT_CLAIMS` cites a reading by its id (the backquoted name in its heading here, or in
`docs/step2-evidence/readings.md`); `test.js` resolves every id to its register entry and checks that the source named is the entry's (a key of
`APPEARANCE_SOURCES`: a work described only in this register's table is added there when a claim first cites it), that "fact" rests on a
reading labelled fact and read wholly on page images, and that "attributed" rests on a reading read wholly on page images. A negative
(`C<k>.n<n>`) is not a heading: an "uncertain" claim cites the sources searched, each with its locator.

**Outside the inventory, and why.** Names and identifiers; enums and keys; `tm.ev` (a quotation of another statement, whose claim is its
own); `tolWhy` (never shown); the apparatus (`CLAIM`, `STATUS`, `NATION`); the appearance table (claim by claim since Stage 6B); the arrows',
lines' and roads' labels (`OVERLAYS`, `TERRAIN_LINES`, `ROADS`: an arrow's claim is its own mark, derived or interpretive, held by
`binding-test.js`); the grade texts (`CONF_TEXT`, `TIMING_TEXT`, `CONF_INTERP`, `POS_CLAIM`, `SRC_LABEL`: definitions, guarded since step 2);
and numbers (strengths, event windows and anchor times, `KNOW_OVERRIDE`), which keep their own grades: the strengths' ranges and notes, the
tracks' timing grades and the chronology ledger (`tools/stage2/chronology.js`), and "Whose eyes?". The presentation sentences of `app.js`
that say what the sources say are reworded in the commits whose readings make them stale.
