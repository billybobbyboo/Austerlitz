## Topic 1: Artillery at Austerlitz (French, Russian, Austrian)

### 1a. Pieces and carriages in use, and the facts that set their shape

1. **The Grande Armée of 1805 took the field with Gribeauval matériel: 12-, 8- and 4-pounder guns and 6-inch howitzers. The an XI pieces (6-pounder; howitzer of 5 pouces 6 lignes, "de 24") were not generalised.**
   - Status: READ (text).
   - Source: Alombert and Colin, *La campagne de 1805 en Allemagne*, t. I (1902), Introduction, pp. 228-229. archive.org id `la-campagne-de-1805-en-allemagne-vol.-1` (leaves n239-n240).
   - URL: https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-de-1805-en-allemagne-vol.-1
   - Quote: « L'artillerie de la Grande Armée comprend des canons de 12, de 8 et de 4, et des obusiers de 6 pouces, du modèle de Gribeauval. D'après le système de l'an xi, les pièces de 8 et de 4 auraient dû être remplacées par des pièces de 6 […] mais le modèle de ces dernières, établi trop hâtivement et mal étudié, avait été rectifié, et la fabrication était peu avancée ; de même, les obusiers de 6 pouces auraient dû être remplacés par des obusiers de 5 pouces 6 lignes, ou de 24 »; p. 229: « la fabrication des affûts et caissons pour ce calibre n'était pas non plus assez avancée pour en pourvoir toute l'armée ».
   - Gloss: the army's artillery was Gribeauval 12, 8, 4 and 6-inch howitzers. The an XI 6-pounder and 24-pounder howitzer were meant to replace them, but production was too little advanced.
   - Establishes: the matériel standard at the start of the campaign, from the Section historique, which worked from the archives.
   - Does not establish: which pieces IV Corps or the Guard actually had on 2 December.
   - Grade: B.

2. **A French infantry division had 12 pieces (2 × 12, 6 × 8, 2 × 4, 2 howitzers) and a cavalry division 2 × 8 and 1 howitzer. Once the army was complete it also had 52 six-pounders and 6 howitzers "de 24".**
   - Status: READ (text).
   - Source: Alombert I, p. 230.
   - Quote: « on eut, pour 223,000 hommes effectifs […] 58 canons de 12, 146 de 8, 52 de 6, 56 de 4, 12 de 3, 2 de 1, 58 obusiers de 6 pouces, 6 obusiers de 24, et 6 obusiers bavarois de 7 livres »; « Dans chaque division d'infanterie, l'artillerie comprenait 12 bouches à feu : 2 de 12, 6 de 8, 2 de 4, et 2 obusiers. Dans chaque division de cavalerie : 2 pièces de 8 et 1 obusier. Les 24 pièces de la Garde […] ».
   - Establishes: the planned divisional mix (B).
   - Does not establish: where the 52 six-pounders served. They could be an XI pieces or captured Austrian 6-pounders; this is open.
   - Grade: B.

3. **The Guard artillery was ordered in three divisions, each of 4 × 8-pounders, 2 × 4-pounders and 2 howitzers.**
   - Status: READ (image).
   - Source: Napoleon to Mortier, Boulogne, 11 fructidor an XIII (29 August 1805), printed in Alombert I, p. 399 (leaf n410).
   - Quote: « l'artillerie de ma Garde sera organisée et attelée de la manière suivante : elle sera divisée en trois divisions ; chaque division sera composée de 4 pièces de 8, 2 pièces de 4 et 2 obusiers, attelés par 44 chevaux ».
   - Establishes: the Guard's intended matériel, 24 pieces, all Gribeauval calibres. This matches Alombert I p. 230's "24 pièces de la Garde".
   - Does not establish: what the Guard brought onto the field on 2 December. The project's strengthNote already records that disagreement.
   - Grade: A (dated order of 1805, printed 1902).

4. **The an XI field carriage (decree of 12 floréal an XI) was judged a failure. The Emperor kept the existing carriages "provisionally", and the 12-pounder carriage was not to change. The 6-pounder and the 24-pounder howitzer were new pieces with new carriages.**
   - Status: READ (image).
   - Source: gassendi1809, t. I, p. 11, note (1) (leaf n92).
   - URL: https://archive.org/download/india.history.resource.106008/page/n92.jpg
   - Quote: « L'arrêté du 12 floréal an 11 a changé l'affût de campagne ; mais comme il a fait le contraire de l'améliorer […] et que Sa Majesté a fait conserver provisoirement les affûts existans, que celui de 12 ne sera pas changé, on n'a pas supprimé leur nomenclature, on a seulement ajouté celle des affûts de 6 et d'obusiers de 24, bouches à feu nouvelles, auxquels il faudra encore changer l'avant-train et le coffret ».
   - Establishes: in 1809 the Gribeauval field carriages were still the army's standard.
   - Grade: B.

5. **Shape difference: in the an XI system the ammunition chest (coffret) rides on the limber. In the Gribeauval field carriage it travels between the cheeks (flasques) of the gun carriage, and the gunners move it onto the limber when they unlimber.**
   - Status, an XI part: READ (image). Source: gassendi1809 t. I p. 19 (leaf n100).
     - Quote: « Avant-train de Campagne, commun au 12, 6, et Obusier de 24. […] 1 Coffret, son couvercle. Ce coffret est placé sur l'avant-train : son derrière s'appuie sur le devant de la grande selette, et son devant contre la petite selette. Sa longueur est de 2 pieds 7 pouces 3 lignes, sa largeur de 1 pied 4 pouces 3 lignes […] sa hauteur est de 1 pied 5 pouces 2 lignes ».
     - Dimensions converted: 0.85 × 0.44 × 0.47 m (INFERENCE).
   - Status, Gribeauval part: READ (text). Source: gassendi1809 t. II (archive.org id `india.history.resource.105837`), pp. 869-870, « Otez l'Avant-train » and « Amenez l'Avant-train ».
     - Quotes: « Canonnier de droite, Canonnier de gauche, placent le coffret sur l'avant-train » (unlimbering), and « Canonnier de droite, Canonnier de gauche placent le coffret entre les flasques » (limbering).
     - Also t. I p. 174 (leaf n255; the OCR running head "295" is a misreading), the field-carriage coffret: « Anneau rond […] derrière le Coffret de 8 seulement, pour le tenir sur les armons de l'avant-train quand on tire à la prolonge ; parce que ce Coffret étant plus long, ne peut, comme les autres, se loger entre les armons ».
   - Shows: a Gribeauval piece on the march has its chest between the cheeks. In action the chest is on the limber, sitting between the limber hounds (armons).
   - Grade: B.

8. **The prolonge (drag rope).**
   - Status: READ (image).
   - Source: gassendi1809 t. I p. 179 (leaf n260).
   - Quote: « Longueur totale, 44 pieds ; diamètre, 11 lignes. […] Il faut 11 pieds de la Prolonge pour l'attacher aux pitons de l'avant-train. Le reste de la longueur est ordinairement replié derrière la sassoire […] Lorsqu'on veut faire traîner le canon en retraite, à la Prolonge, on passe la clef dans l'anneau d'embrelage, on la passe ensuite dans l'un des anneaux de la Prolonge pour la réduire à la moitié ou aux 2 tiers de sa longueur ».
   - Gloss: the rope is ≈ 14.3 m long and 2.5 cm thick. When idle it is coiled behind the limber's sassoire.
   - A second statement elsewhere in Gassendi: t. II p. 869 (READ, text): « La Prolonge entière sera de 24 pieds : raccourcie, de 16 : doublée, de 12 ». This is the working length between limber and trail once the rope has been tied off. Keep both statements together.
   - Grade: B.

9. **Russian field matériel of the Arakcheev period.**
   - Status: READ (p. 21 on the image; p. 20 on the text layer).
   - Source (new): А. А. Нилус, *История материальной части артиллерии*, т. 2, СПб 1904, pp. 20-21. archive.org id `nilusaaistorijamaterialnojchastiartil76`, leaves n23-n24.
   - URL: https://archive.org/download/nilusaaistorijamaterialnojchastiartil76/page/n24.jpg
   - What Nilus says (gloss; full quotes below):
     - Companies: battery companies have 12-pounder guns (medium and small proportion) and ½-pood unicorns, 4 of each kind, 12 per company. Light companies have 8 six-pounders and 4 ¼-pood unicorns. Horse companies have 6 of each.
     - Carriage: a lightened, two-cheek wooden carriage, with the cheeks diverging towards the trail, joined by 3 transoms. The carriage wheels are larger than the limber wheels. Elevation is by a wedge moved by a horizontal screw. The trail has a folding handspike (2 on battery carriages). Some carriages have a box between the cheeks.
     - Limber: two patterns, battery and light, each with a box over the axle and a pole. Battery limbers carried implements only; light limbers had cells for 20 or 12 rounds. Axles were wooden.
     - Ammunition vehicle: one pattern, a two-wheeled "Petrovsky" box with a flat lid. It replaced Paul I's four-wheeled wagons.
     - Harness: Paul I's breast-harness was replaced by collars.
     - One forge per company. The regimental guns were returned to the regiments, ½ light company per regiment.
   - Quotes:
     - p. 20: «облегченный двухстанинный деревянный, по прежнему съ расходящимися къ хоботу станинами».
     - p. 21: «Передки съ коробомъ и дышломъ имѣлись двухъ образцовъ, батарейный и легкій — съ коробами на оси, а зарядный ящикъ одинъ двухколесный Петровскій, но съ плоскою крышею … Оси остались деревянными … Батарейные передки возили только принадлежность, но не имѣли боевыхъ припасовъ … Легкіе передки были снабжены клѣтками для боевыхъ припасовъ (20 и 12) … Введенная Павломъ I шорная упряжь была замѣнена хомутами».
   - Establishes: the shapes, and that the Russian piece had a bracket (two-cheek) trail like the French.
   - Does not establish:
     - That every piece was of this pattern on 2 December (see the next item). Nilus also says (p. 20) that the "system 1805" carriages lasted to 1845.
     - Team sizes.
     - Colours.
   - Grade: B.

    - Source (new): К. Г. Игошин, «Система Аракчеева и чертежи полевой артиллерии 1805 года», in *Война и оружие*, Труды IV конференции, СПб 2013, ч. II, pp. 206-223.

      - The label appears in 1851 (Vessel).
      - The report "О штате артиллерийских батальонов" approved on 19 March 1803 declared the need to give up ammunition wagons, return to ammunition boxes (whose design was still to be made) and replace breast-harness with collars.
    - Quote, p. 211: «декларирование необходимости отказаться от зарядных фур и вернуться к зарядным ящикам (конструкцию которых еще предстояло разработать), заменить шорную запряжку хомутовой».

72. **The moon on the night of 1-2 December 1805 was about 10 days old, waxing gibbous, about 78 % lit.**

## Summary

1. **SOURCED, French:** Gribeauval 12, 8 and 4 and the 6-inch howitzer (Alombert I; Guard order of 29 August 1805, A). Bracket-trail carriages. Wheels 4 pi 6 po (≈ 1.46 m) for the guns and 3 pi 6 po for the limbers. The coffret between the cheeks on the march and on the limber in action. A 44-foot prolonge. Team lengths and frontage (Gassendi, B). Drivers were train soldiers, mounted on the near horse, one per vehicle in IV Corps in October 1805 (A). Carriages olive with black ironwork (B).
2. **SOURCED, Austrian:** Liechtenstein matériel to 1809, with yellow woodwork and black ironwork (Teuber p. 185, B). This lifts the Austrian colour out of "not found". Battalion guns still in 1805. Teams of 2, 4 and 6 by calibre. Fuhrwesen drivers in the saddle. The cavalry gun's Wurst seat (B; the howitzer team disagreement kept).
3. **SOURCED, Russian shapes** (Nilus 1904, B): a two-cheek trail diverging to the rear, a limber with a box over the axle and a pole, a two-wheeled flat-lidded ammunition box, collar harness, drivers from the company's own men (1806). **GENERIC:** Russian colours and team sizes (only C sources).
4. **SOURCED, villages** (cadastre 1825-1826, B, 20 years late): long street-greens with narrow, deep plots; dwellings drawn pink (fireproof, masonry) and set end-on or L-shaped to the street; timber barns drawn yellow across the back of the yards at Prace; orchards behind. Fences and ditches round Telnitz and Sokolnitz (Schönhals, B). House counts from 1793 (Schwoy) and 1836-37 (Wolny).
5. **GENERIC, villages:** roofing and roof form. No source for 1805. The fire record suggests combustible roofs (INFERENCE).
6. **SOURCED, landmarks** (B): the Sokolnitz castle quadrangle (2-storey east front), its chapel (1750), walled orchard, 4-storey granary and pheasant garden of 90-100 Joch (1837). The Telnitz church (1726-1740) and Pratzen church (1728-1730, on the hill outside the village). The Santon chapel: standing from 1700, destroyed in the battle, rebuilt 1832. The Slavkov church (1786-1789) and château. Blaziowitz castle.
7. **OPEN, landmarks:** the pheasantry wall's date. The St Anthony chapel's roof in 1805. The Santon chapel's form (Marbot's "minaret", C). Whether Kovalovice č.p. 109 is the 1805 post house; it lies about 1.4 km from the project's point.
8. **SOURCED, water** (B): the Satschan pond of about 183 ha (1836); the Menitz pond of about 690 ha, full in 1805 and drained from 1824, with 4 hay islands (1793); the Kobelnitz pond, the Sokolnitz ponds and the Wazan pond (drained 1827) all existed. The Goldbach valley was marshy. The meres were frozen; how strongly is disputed (Stutterheim "not strong enough" against Schönhals "very firmly").
9. **SOURCED, weather:** fog in the valleys at dawn, lifting from about 7-8; then sun (A as the Bulletin's claim, B/C as memoirs); muddy roads in the afternoon. Prague's instrumental series shows mild days from 30 November to 2 December (A for Prague, C for Austerlitz). The eyewitnesses' "extremely cold" night, and their evening snow against freezing rain, stay in conflict.
10. **Next reads** to close the gaps: the 1824 Cadastral Instruction; the First Military Survey sheets; the ČÚZK imperial imprints for Újezd, Žatčany and Měnín; Dolleczek 1887; Igoshin's 1805 drawings; Gassendi t. II pp. 829 and 869-874 on the images; the Vienna daily series.
