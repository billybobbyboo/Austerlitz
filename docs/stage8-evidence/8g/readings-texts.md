# Register of readings: the battlefield villages' buildings and landmarks around 1805 (texts)

> **Provenance.** Compiled 28-29 October 2026 by a research reader working for this session (a **machine reader**; no person read
> these pages). Nothing in the repository was changed. Every quotation below was copied from the **page image** (archive.org page
> images, the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek's IIIF images, ULB Tirol's page images, archive.org IIIF); the text layer (OCR) was used only
> to find pages, and where it disagreed with the image the image wins (the disagreements found are listed in section T7). The images
> quoted from are saved in `img/` beside this file (full leaves, crops, and `check_*` zooms used to read digits). The leads register
> (`docs/stage8-evidence/leads.md`, topic 2, items 34-57) was re-read on the images; corrections are in section T7.

## How to read this register

- **Entry**: the source key (a key of `appearance.js` `APPEARANCE_SOURCES` where the work is registered; new works are described in
  `SOURCES.md` and need registering), the locator (printed page and scan leaf or canvas), the image URLs, the saved image, the passage as
  printed, a translation, the grade and label, what it settles and what it does not, what in the map it bears on, and its status.
- **Grades** (the brief's proposal, for the use of drawing 1805): **A** only a document dated 1804-1805; **B** a later period document
  or a specialist work consistent with the period (Schwoy 1793, Wolny 1836-37 and his church topography 1856-61, participant memoirs
  and official histories); **C** late, modern or unsourced. Three departures from the earlier registers, stated where they occur:
  Stutterheim's account (printed 1806 by an officer present) is graded **B** here, though the step-4 register grades it **A** on its own
  scale; Wolny 1836 is graded **B** here, though the step-4 register grades its entries **C**; the 1824 cadastral Instruction is graded
  **A for the meaning of the cadastre's colours** only (it is the regulation the 1825-26 sketches were drawn under) and says nothing
  about 1805.
- **Labels**: fact (what the source says, as it says it), attributed (what the source reports from another witness, named), disputed
  (another source read here says otherwise; both kept), inference (the reader's own reasoning, always marked), uncertain.
- **Transcription**: as printed, with the source's spelling, abbreviations and capitals; German Fraktur long s as s; the Fraktur sign
  for et cetera as "ꝛc."; a word divided at a line end joined; "[...]" an omission; "/" a page or column break; "[sic]" where the print
  is odd; nothing silently corrected.
- **Units** (inference, for orientation only): 1 Vienna Joch = 0.5755 ha; 1 Vienna Klafter = 1.8965 m; 1 Moravian/Austrian Meile
  about 7.6 km and the topographies' "Stunde" (Wegstunde) about half a Meile.
- **For 1836-37 and 1856-61 texts** each entry says whether the thing described can be assumed for 1805: it is fact for 1805 only where
  the text prints a date before 1805 (a building date, a fire); otherwise applying it to 1805 is an inference, labelled so, with what
  supports it (usually that nothing read records a change in between).

## Findings at a glance

Established (fact as the sources state it; the entries give the grade):

- **House counts** of every village of the field in 1793 (twelve years before the battle) and in 1836-37 (the table below). Augezd had
  burnt down entirely in 1794 but for its church (T1.16); Pratze was partly burnt in 1805 (T1.7); Blaziowitz suffered fire and plunder in
  the battle (T1.18); the Telnitz parsonage's farm buildings burnt after it (T3.8).
- **Churches** stood in 1805 at Telnitz, Pratzen, Augezd, Satschan, Menitz, Bosenitz, Křenowitz, Schlapanitz, Posořitz and Austerlitz,
  and a chapel inside the Sokolnitz castle. **None** is named, in 1793, 1836-37 or 1856-61, at Girzikowitz, Kobelnitz, Puntowitz,
  Blaziowitz, Holubitz, Kruh, Klein-Hostiehradek or Kowalowitz, nor in Sokolnitz village (Telnitz parish). Pratzen's church stood
  **outside the village on a hill** (1793 and later); Telnitz's a little apart from the village in a **walled churchyard** that the French
  defended in the battle; Křenowitz's on a hill east of the village; Menitz's at the north end; Satschan's north of the village; Augezd's
  in the middle. (world.js `CHURCHES` draws churches at Girzikowitz, Kobelnitz, Blasowitz, Holubitz and Sokolnitz village, all at village
  centres, and none at Satschan or Menitz.)
- **The chapel above Augezd** was St Anthony of Padua's, built before 1713 by the parish priest, deconsecrated, still standing in 1818,
  demolished afterwards (T3.1). **The Santon's chapel** was half masonry and half wood and was destroyed by the French in the battle
  (T1.8, T3.11; its dates disputed between Wolny's two works).
- **Sokolnitz castle** (1837): a closed quadrangle on a rise, the east wing of two storeys, the other three of one, with the chapel in
  them; a walled orchard to the west running into a pheasant garden of 90-100 Joch with an avenue of old horse chestnuts and a brook;
  farm, four-storey granary, fish pond and a poplar avenue of about 400 Klafter to the village (T1.1, T1.2). In 1805 Thiébault fought
  through its avenues, stables, barns, outbuildings and main house (T6.6).
- **Village edges**: Telnitz ringed by vineyards and ditches (Stutterheim 1806), Telnitz and Sokolnitz "mit Zäunen und Gräben umgeben"
  (Schönhals 1873).
- **The cadastre's colours** (the 1824 Instruction): masonry ("gemauert") pale carmine, public buildings strong carmine, wooden
  ("hölzern") pale yellow; **walls only, no roof rule** (T4.1, N4.1).
- **Roofs**: the town of Austerlitz tiled by 1792 after a fire of about 1772 (T2.15); the Bosenitz parsonage tiled in 1773 (T3.12);
  **old thatched roofs** in the French bivouacs (Lejeune, T5.1). Nothing names the roofs of the field's village houses (T5, N1.1, N5.1).

Disputed or uncertain: the Santon chapel's chronology (T3.11); the Sokolnitz chapel's altars, 8 or 3 (T1.1, T3.9); the Telnitz church's
rebuilding, 1726-1734 or 1726-1740 (T3.7); the Telnitz school's fire, about 1805 or 1808 (T3.6); the Austerlitz portico's columns, Ionic
or Corinthian (T3.13); the village Lejeune saw burnt (T5.5); Schönhals's "westlich" (T6.1).

Not found: the roof material of any battlefield village's houses; any fire at the Sokolnitz castle in 1805; the St Anthony chapel's
state in 1805; a post at Posoritz before 1836 in the topographies (NEGATIVES.md).

### T1.1 `wolny1837.p465.sokolnitz-schloss`

- **source**: `wolny1837`, p. 465 (leaf n470), the sentence ending on p. 466 (n471); images:
  https://archive.org/download/10011828bsb/page/n470.jpg, https://archive.org/download/10011828bsb/page/n471.jpg; saved:

- **quote** (read on: page image): „Ortbeschreibung. 1. Der Amtsort Sokolnitz (Sokolnice), liegt 2 Stund. sö. von Brünn in einer
  sanften Vertiefung, und gewährt mit seinen malerischen Umgebungen einen reizenden Anblick. Das obrgktl. Schloß steht auf einer
  Anhöhe und bildet ein regelmäßiges Viereck, dessen östl. Fronte 2 Stockwerke hoch ist und geräumige Wohnungen für die gräfl.
  Obrigkeit enthält, während die andern 3 Seiten nur 1 Stockwerk haben, und theils die vom Gf. Leopold v. Dietrichstein erbaute, 1750
  zur öffentlichen Kirche und der Erhöhung des hl. Kreuzes geweihte Schloßkapelle mit 8 Altären, in der ein eigens von der Obrigkeit
  gestifteter Seelsorger den Gottesdienst verrichtet, theils die gesammten Kanzleien, nebst der Wohnung des Oberbeamten und des
  Kanzleipersonals einschließen. An dasselbe stoßt im W. der mit seltenen Weinreben, Pfirsich- und andern edlen Bäumen besetzte und
  ummauerte Obstgarten an, welcher gegen N. in den 90 bis 100 Joch betragenden Fasangarten ausläuft, der durch seine Gänge und Gruppen
  von Bäumen, besonders aber durch die breite Hauptallee von hochbejahrten wilden Kastanienbäumen und den ihn durchschlängelnden / Bach,
  einen sehr anmuthigen Lustort darbietet."
- **translation**: The seat Sokolnitz lies 2 hours SE of Brünn in a gentle hollow. The manorial castle stands on a rise and forms a
  regular quadrangle, whose east front is two storeys high and holds spacious apartments for the count's family, while the other three
  sides have only one storey and contain partly the castle chapel built by Count Leopold Dietrichstein and consecrated in 1750 as a
  public church of the Exaltation of the Holy Cross, "with 8 altars", served by a priest endowed by the lordship, and partly all the
  offices with the dwellings of the chief official and the office staff. On the west adjoins the walled orchard planted with rare vines,
  peaches and other fine trees, which runs out northward into the pheasant garden of 90 to 100 Joch, a most pleasant place with its
  walks and groups of trees, above all its broad main avenue of very old horse chestnuts and the brook winding through it.
- **grade**: B. **label**: fact; the altar count disputed (KT 1856 p. 464: 3 altars, T3.9).
- **settles**: In 1837 the castle was a closed four-sided block on a rise, the east wing two storeys, the others one storey (the chapel
  and the offices in them); a **walled orchard** on the west ran north into a pheasant garden of 90-100 Joch (inference: about 52-58 ha)
  with a main avenue of old horse chestnuts and a winding brook.
- **does not settle**: The castle's form in 1805: Wolny gives no building history of the castle, and no fire or rebuilding in or after
  1805 (searched, NEGATIVES N1.3). For 1805 the quadrangle with its chapel wing is an **inference** (the chapel was consecrated in 1750 and
  enlarged by then, T3.9; the 1825 indication sketch shows a closed block round a court, leads item 37; nothing read records a change
  between 1805 and 1837). Whether the pheasant garden itself was walled (only the orchard is "ummauert"). Roofing (not mentioned).
  "8 Altären" is printed so (checked at 4x); KT 1856 says the chapel has 3 altars since 1750: probably a misprint, uncertain.
- **bears on**: FEATURES[#sokolnitz].sub and .why ("a walled castle and a pheasantry enclosure"); world.js the Sokolnitz castle block
  ("three wings round a court, and a taller block at the corner") and WOODS' "Sokolnitz pheasantry"; leads items 37, 48, 49.
- **status**: read on page image by a machine reader, 29 October 2026; checked against the text layer (which also has "8 Altären").
